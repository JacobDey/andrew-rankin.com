# LATENT IMAGE: The Kessane Hold (story bible)

*Game 4, slug `latent`. Companion to `docs/design/latent.md` (the GDD). ## 1. Logline

March 2003. The police Photographic Unit in the river city of Varrow shuts down at dawn. You are the night contractor hired to develop and index one carton before it is destroyed: eleven rolls of film, ten of them never developed, left on Shelf 4 since 1979 under the label **HOLD — R.K.** The work order was filed by a records clerk called **R. Kessane**. The rolls belonged to a police photographer called **Ruth Kessane**, who resigned in 1979 and was never seen again.

The darkroom tells you three things in turn. A senior officer burned two buildings for insurance money. The woman buried in 1979 under the name **Mina Lusk** was really Ruth. And the person who sent you here tonight is Mina, who has lived for twenty-four years under the name of the woman she loved, and who cannot accuse anyone without confessing who she is.

**Theme:** a photograph remembers who was holding the camera. **Feeling at the end:** grief, then a decision that is entirely yours.

## 2. Setting

- **Varrow.** A fictional, rain-heavy river city with a cannery past. In 1979 its streets are lit by low-pressure sodium lamps, which are nearly pure 589 nm orange, so the city at night is already monochrome. The river is the **Sallow**. The old industrial bank is **the Flats**.
- **Ferry Street Station.** The central police station. The **Photographic Unit** occupies the basement: an outer office with a records terminal (installed in 1988, amber screen), a light-table room, a darkroom with a film-loading closet, and the **cold store** (4 °C) where undeveloped and latent material is held.
- **Tonight:** Friday 28 March 2003, 21:00 to 06:00. On Monday the department switches to digital and the Unit is closed. The silver-recovery contractor collects everything on Shelf 4 at 06:00. The heating in the basement is already off.
- **The player:** an unnamed night technician from **Calder Records Services**, a records agency. You have never been to Varrow. That is why you were chosen.

### Regulation 14.2 (the engine of the plot)
Varrow Police General Orders, Reg. 14.2: *Latent (undeveloped) photographic material may not be destroyed until it has been processed and indexed by a party with no connection to the case. The processing party shall record a disposition: FILE (refer to Professional Standards) or PURGE (destroy).* Mina knows this rule because Ruth once complained about it over dinner.

### Why Mina can't simply speak
If she comes forward she has to explain whose name she has used for twenty-four years, whose body lies under her name at St. Ebba's, and why she ran. Her word against a Deputy Chief's, with no physical evidence, would bury her and not him. Reg. 14.2 is her way to put the evidence in front of a stranger who has no reason to lie. She also wants someone, once, to look at the pictures of Ruth.

### What the game never states
- That Ruth and Mina were lovers. Rolls 5 and 10 make it plain; no document says it.
- That R. Kessane in 2003 is Mina. Roll 11 frame 36, the contract record, the cursive "START HERE" card and the final fax make it plain; nobody says it.
- Whether filing is right. Filing exposes Hale and also exposes Mina's twenty-four-year fraud. Purging protects Mina and lets Hale retire with a medal. The game does not judge either choice.

## 5. The eleven rolls: every frame

### 5.1 Staging conventions (for the diorama builder)
- **Units:** metres. World +Y up. Ground at Y = 0. Every frame is a 35 mm frame (36 × 24 mm, 3:2) shot with a **50 mm lens**: horizontal FOV 39.6°, vertical FOV 27.0°. Unless noted, the camera is **level** (pitch 0, roll 0), which matters for the horizon clues.
- **Camera heights:** Ruth **1.66 m** (eye 1.69, height 1.80). Mina **1.46 m** (eye 1.49, height 1.60). Copy stand: 0.55 m above the baseboard, looking straight down.
- **Scene luminance:** linear L, 0–1. Paper-white subject 0.80, mid-grey card 0.18, night street 0.005–0.05, sodium lamp core 1.0+ (clipped). Each detail lists its own luminance and that of its immediate surround. These are design targets: tune the lights until a luminance probe reads within ±20%.
- **Exposure offset (EV):** applied at the camera. Ruth's brackets are −1 / 0 / +1. Mina's +2 rolls are shot at **−2 EV** (EI 1600 on 400 film).
- **Frame coordinates:** positive orientation (as printed), origin top-left, x right, y down, both 0–1. `bbox = [x, y, w, h]`.
- **The gate tooth:** every frame shot in Ruth's camera (all eleven rolls) has a small clear notch on the negative at the top-right film edge (x = 0.93), just outside the image area. It shows at the light table, and on prints made at M ≤ 3.5, where the black carrier border falls on the paper.
- **Detail patterns** (see GDD §5.7): BURIED (in shadow), BLOWN (in highlight), SMALL (too few print cells), MIRRORED (needs carrier flip), GHOST (faint density), PUSHED (lost unless the roll was push-developed), OFFFRAME (on the film edge or cassette, read at the light table).
- **Legibility thresholds** per detail: `rho` (correlation, default 0.5) and `mMin` (minimum magnification so a glyph is ≥ 7 print cells tall). The GDD defines the metric.
- **Bracketed scenes:** "F2–4 (−1/0/+1)" means one composition rendered three times at different EV. Brackets cost nothing to make and are canonical character detail.
- **Figures** are low-poly: a capsule torso, a box head and cylinder limbs, scaled to height. Silhouette carries identity:
  - **Ruth:** tall, short hair, long dark coat, camera strap wrapped in white tape.
  - **Mina:** small, chin-length bob, short pale jacket.
  - **Hale:** tall, trench coat, flat cap.
  - **Dunmore:** bald, heavy, wide coat, briefcase.
  - **Rusk:** thin, glasses, bow tie.
  - **Dot:** small, cardigan, glasses on a chain.
  - **Atwell:** very tall, young, uniform.

### 5.3 Roll 1: Holloway's Chandlery (the practice roll)
This roll teaches developing. It carries almost no story, but it plants two habits (the slate and the brackets) and one image.

- **F1 Slate.** Ruth's hand holds a black chalk slate filling the centre 60% of the frame against a shop's brick pier. Chalk, block capitals, 3 lines: `79-0108` / `8.1.79` / `R.K.` Slate L 0.04, chalk 0.55. Her hand at the left edge, with the white-taped strap looping below it (L 0.6).
- **F2–4 (−1/0/+1) Shopfront.** Quay Road, overcast morning. Camera 1.66 m, 6 m back from a narrow shop, straight on. A fascia board across the top third carries painted letters `HOLLOWAY'S CHANDLERY` (L 0.75 on 0.10). Smaller window lettering below: `ROPE · TAR · LAMP OIL · CHARTS`. The lower-left pane is smashed: a star of white cracks (L 0.7) over a black interior (0.02). Rain-dark pavement fills the bottom fifth.
- **F5–7 (−1/0/+1) Counter.** Interior: a till drawer pulled out empty, a brass ship's lamp on the counter, a 15 cm steel rule beside a muddy boot smear on the counter top.
- **F8–10 (−1/0/+1) Jemmy mark.** Close-up, camera 0.6 m from a painted door edge with a pry scar, steel rule beside it; rule numerals `1 2 3 … 15` legible at M ≥ 5.
- **F11 The shadow.** Low winter sun behind the photographer. Pavement, kerb, a puddle, and across them one very long shadow of a tall woman with a camera to her face, the strap hanging in a loop. Nothing in the game comments on it. It is the first picture of Ruth, and it is only a shadow.

- **Details:** none load-bearing. Searchable words a player will find: `HOLLOWAY`, `CHANDLERY`, `QUAY`.

### 5.5 Roll 3: Tanner's Row, the collision (Ruth's camera height)
- **F1 Slate.** `79-0122` / `22.1.79` / `R.K.`
- **F2–4 (−1/0/+1) The alley.** **The most important composition in the game.** Camera at **1.66 m**, perfectly level, centred in a straight brick alley 3.0 m wide and 40 m long, looking down its length (one-point perspective, vanishing point at image centre).
  - Both walls are brick at **75 mm per course** (65 mm brick, 10 mm mortar), with visible mortar lines.
  - Painted along both walls, with its top edge at exactly **1.52 m**, runs a 5 cm **white flood line** (L 0.75 on brick 0.12).
  - On the left wall, 4 m from the camera, hangs a 30 × 20 cm cast plaque, top edge at 1.52 m: `FLOOD LEVEL · MARCH 1961`.
  - A grey delivery van has nosed into the left wall 6 m away, below the plaque, its bonnet crumpled.
  - At the far end, a lit street (L 0.5) frames the vanishing point.
  - Because the camera is above 1.52 m, the white flood lines lie **below** the vanishing point and **rise** toward it on both walls.
  - **Detail `HORIZON_RUTH`**: bbox `[0.20, 0.40, 0.60, 0.25]` (both flood lines and the vanishing point). M ≥ 2 (readable full-frame). The plaque text is a SMALL sub-detail: bbox `[0.05, 0.42, 0.12, 0.08]`, M ≥ 6.
- **F5–7 (−1/0/+1) The van.** Three-quarter view of the crumpled bonnet, registration `VRN 2207`, a cracked headlamp. Harmless: searching the plate returns only the 79-0122 report.
- **F8–10 (−1/0/+1) Skid marks.** Looking down at two black skid marks curving toward the wall, steel rule for scale.
- **F11 The plaque.** Close and straight on: `FLOOD LEVEL · MARCH 1961` with a brass benchmark arrow under it. A gift for players who can't read the plaque in F2–4.

### 5.7 Roll 5: Personal (the heart)
No slate, no brackets, every frame unique. Hand-held at Ruth's height except F10.

- **F1 Line 2.** Sallow Valley Photofinishing at night: a long processing machine (a row of tanks under a hanging rack). Mina in a hairnet and work coat, mid-laugh, turning from the machine. On the wall above her: `SALLOW VALLEY PHOTOFINISHING · LINE 2 · NO NAKED LIGHTS`.

## 6. The records database (final text)

### 6.1 Rules the writing obeys
- Every document is searchable by **every word** in its header and body (rules in GDD §5.9). Searches return **at most 5 documents, oldest first**, plus the total count.
- Two voices. **Police prose:** terse, upper case, abbreviations (`DOA`, `NFA`, `SUBJ`, `VEH`, `RTA`, `F&I`). **Margins:** Dot's notes are lower case, after `D.P.`. People slip only in the margins.
- Words that exist **only in photographs** are never printed in a document unless a document *also* holds them as a lead. `KDL 417` appears in the registry extract, but nothing that leads there says "KDL". You only find the registry by reading a plate.
- Documents are listed below in date order. Format: **`ID`** · date (ISO, used for sorting) · type. Then the body exactly as shown in game. `[[...]]` marks text typed as redaction blocks (████), which is not searchable.

## 9. Red herrings (fair, and each resolved)
1. **"Ruth is alive and filed the order."** Everyone believes it at first: KESSANE returns a 2003 contract with Ruth's date of birth. Deductions 7–12 overturn it, and that reversal is the game's big turn.
2. **Peter Odell.** He asked in 1980 for Shelf 4 to be cleared, which looks sinister. His captions are honest and complete ("drawer open and empty"); he wanted shelf space. Dot said no.
3. **Gil Dunmore as the arsonist.** He paid, and he profited, but his car never appears; the plate and the heel are Hale's.
4. **Sgt Atwell.** He was first on the scene and he is the voice on the phone, so some players will suspect him. His statement, his margin note and his unprompted call clear him.
5. **The van `VRN 2207`.** Search it and you get a dull RTA. Not everything is a lead.
6. **`LANTERN`** returns `NO MATCH`. The diner matters to the story, not to the police.
7. **Cyril Odgers.** Cranky, present at the Tannery that night, and innocent. He was photographing herons.

## 10. Difficulty pacing

Stages are progress flags used for hints and for the `__game` test API. They are not hard gates. The only hard gates are physical: a roll has to be developed before it can be printed, and pushed detail needs a push.

| Stage | Flag set when | Typical minute | Feeling |
|---|---|---|---|
| A | First print developed (Roll 7 F1 slate) | 0–3 | "Oh, it appears." |
| B | `ODGERS` or `4471` searched | 3–6 | "A birdwatcher? On a police roll?" |
| C | First roll developed in the tank | 6–14 | Hands learning. First kinks. |
| D | `KDL 417` searched | 12–25 | "A detective's car behind a fire." |
| E | Any heel detail legible | 18–35 | "And his boot." |
| F | Roll 7 F10 legible | 20–40 | "He did it again, and someone was there." |
| G | Roll 8 or 9 developed with push ≥ +1.5 stops | 30–50 | Craft pays off. |
| H | `STATURE`/`FEMUR`/`HEIGHT` searched or door marks legible | 35–60 | "Wait. She was tall." |
| I | Both horizon frames printed | 45–70 | "Two people held that camera." |
| J | Roll 11 F36 legible | 55–85 | Grief, then the turn: she's alive, and she sent you. |
| K | `CLOSE 03-0417` | 60–100 | The choice. |

**Curve.** Act I (A–C) is all toy, with one hook (an off-duty slate, a birdwatcher). Act II (D–G) is the procedural you expect: a corrupt cop, plates and boots, satisfying and a little familiar. Act III (H–I) turns the expected story over: the case was never about who set the fire but about who died in it. Act IV (J–K) moves it to the present: the person who has been guiding your hands all night, in the handwriting on every card, is the woman everyone thinks is in the grave. Nothing is timed; a strong player finishes in about 60 minutes, a thorough one in 100.

**Fairness checks** (the build's lint script asserts these):
- Every deduction in §8 has two routes that use different rolls or documents.
- No load-bearing detail requires a magnification above 14 or a correlation above 0.5.
- Every roll's essential detail survives any development between 0.7× and 1.4× nominal time, except PUSHED details, which need ≥ 1.35×. Rolls 8 and 9 have unpushed routes to the same deductions (Roll 7, the documents).
- The kink crescents never overlap a detail bbox, whether the developer is Mina or the player.
