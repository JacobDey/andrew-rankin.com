# RUBY SECTOR: Story Bible

*Game 3, slug `plates`. Companion to `plates.md` (the GDD). This file is the single source of truth for every name, date, image, clue and line of in-game text. All persons, places, vessels and institutions are invented. Text in quote blocks marked **FINAL** ships verbatim.*

**Spoiler warning for builders:** this file is the answer key.

---

## 1. Setting

**Gaunt Rock Light**, a granite tower on a bare tidal rock eleven miles off a fictional western coast. It is a *family station*: a tower, and beside it a low stone row of three cottages joined to the tower foot by a walled path. The light is **fixed white with a red sector**. A brass frame holds three panes of ruby glass (**Sector I, II, III**, each 10 × 12 in) in the lantern glazing on the east side. From the sea, a ship that sees red is over **the Teeth**, a reef 60 m east-south-east of the landing. A ship that sees white is in safe water.

**The Board of Sea Lamps** is the invented authority that runs the light. It is procedural, decent and slow, and writes in small capitals.

**Period.** November 1887. Gelatine dry plates are new to amateurs. Unlike wet collodion, a dry plate keeps its *latent* (exposed but undeveloped) image for months. Dry plates are blue-sensitive, so **red things photograph black** and blue sky photographs blank white. Ruby glass is the safe darkroom light. Canary (amber) glass is "safe enough" for fixed glass only.

**The darkroom.** The darkroom is the old coal store at the tower foot. It has a vaulted ceiling, a back wall curved to follow the tower, a deal bench and an iron hook for a lamp. **It is the same room in 1887 and in 1888.** The player works in it, and three plates show it.

**The two times.**
- **11–12 November 1887.** The night the ketch *Annick Rose* struck the Teeth. All plates were exposed between Thursday 10th and Saturday 12th.
- **Tuesday 16 October 1888.** The player arrives. The old tower will be put out when a new tower on the north end of the Rock is lit on 1 November 1888. The rock is otherwise empty except for the cat.

**Theme.** *The same red glass means "safe" in one room and "danger" in the other.* Moved from the lantern to the darkroom, it turned a warning into a welcome. The game is about a lie told for love, and a truth kept in the dark until someone was ready to develop it.

## 2. The player

**The Board's Examiner of Plates**, never named, never seen. The player is not a detective. The Board has sent them because they know the craft. Their only instructions are the Board's covering note and Ottilie Frane's letter. Their tools are the bench, the lamp, the light table and the Board's **Return**.

**After the night** (told only in documents and endings):
- **Penwarden** was dismissed in December 1887 on the Inquiry's findings. He drowned in March 1888 when the fishing smack he had joined was lost. His widow is **Mrs Agnes Penwarden** of Trevennor.
- **Amos** was retired by the Board in May 1888 "on the ground of his sight".
- **Ottilie** died of consumption at **Wendlow** in September 1888. Her letter reached the Board after her death.
- **Nell** is twelve in 1888 and lives ashore with her parents.

### Character notes for the writing

- **Amos** is proud, Methodist and exact. He has kept his failing sight (cataract) from the Board for two years. He loves his daughter more than his Service, and on the night it came to a choice he learned this about himself.
- **Keturah** is practical and quick. She is the first to think of the cold men.
- **Nell** is clever and impulsive. She worshipped Ottilie and loved the work. She meant to put the glass back before dark. She did not put it back because her father went up to the lantern first, and she was afraid of him.
- **Penwarden** is taciturn and decent. He drinks nothing (he took the pledge in the Navy), which is why the brandy charge stung him most. He owes a moneylender £14 because of his wife's illness.
- **Davy** is cheerful, cannot keep still, plays the concertina badly and is kind to the cat. He went into the sea after the boy without a word.
- **Ottilie** is precise, wry and ill (she coughs; she is dying and knows it). She never asked Nell where the glass came from, and she has never forgiven herself.

## 4. The true sequence of events

All times are Gaunt Rock local time. Sunset on 11 Nov is 4.28 p.m., and lighting-up is entered at 4.32 p.m.

**Friday 4 November 1887.** Ottilie lands with her stereoscopic camera, her plates and her darkroom lamp. The lamp is a japanned tin box with a ruby window **10 × 12 in** that slides in grooves. She sets up in the coal store and exposes her TRIAL plate.

**30 September (before she came).** The station's only spare sector pane broke in landing. A replacement has been requisitioned and not delivered. *There is no spare ruby glass on the Rock.*

**Thursday 10 Nov, 11.40 a.m.** Ottilie photographs the station company at the tower door (**plate 1**).

**Friday 11 Nov, 6.05 a.m.** A heavy sea strikes the tower. In the coal store, Ottilie's lamp is thrown off its hook and its ruby window cracks right across. She cannot develop until the relief boat comes, a fortnight off.

**10.15 a.m.** Amos cleans the lens and enters "Sector glasses examined, in order". Nell has followed Ottilie up. She lays her left hand on Sector III and asks whether it is the same size as a lamp window. Amos doesn't answer: he is peering at a prism through his magnifier (**plate 2**).

**11.30 a.m.** Ottilie photographs the darkroom "for the album", with the cracked lamp on the bench. Nell sits on the coal bin and stares at it (**plate 3**).

**c. 12.35 p.m.** While the household gathers for dinner, Nell goes up the tower. She lifts Sector III out of its frame (the panes sit in a rebate and are held by turn-buttons). Her scarf snags on a screw of the empty bracket and leaves a tuft of red wool. She wraps the pane in the scarf and carries it down.

**12.45 p.m.** Dinner. Nell's chair is empty, and Wick sits in it (**plate 4**).

**1.50 p.m.** Ottilie, with a long lens on the landing, photographs the tower against the sky. Pane III's opening shows white sky (**plate 5**). Nobody notices. The gale is getting up.

**c. 2.30 p.m.** Nell gives Ottilie "red glass" in a scarf. It fits the lamp's grooves exactly. Ottilie does not ask where it came from.

**3.15 p.m.** Ottilie photographs Nell, bare-necked and proud, beside the mended lamp on its hook. The Board's etch on the pane's lower edge, `B.S.L. · GAUNT ROCK · SECTOR III`, catches a little light from the door (**plate 6**). Then they work. Ottilie develops two of her earlier plates from the 7th (not in the tin; she later destroyed them).

**4.32 p.m.** Amos goes up to light up. Nell follows to the hatch, meaning to confess and put the glass back. Ottilie is on the gallery photographing the lighting-up (**plate 7**). Amos stands beside the empty frame and does not see it: his eyes are failing. Nell sees her father, loses her nerve and goes back down. The light is lit. Over the Teeth, the red sector now has a **white slot** in its middle.

**c. 6.40 p.m.** The *Annick Rose* (Hosken, master) runs for the Rock's lee in the gale. She keeps the red sector on her quarter as Hosken knows it. As she closes, she comes into the white slot: "the red went white on us." Hosken believes he has passed the Teeth, and stands in.

**c. 6.58 p.m.** Penwarden, off watch, sees her lights from his cottage window and knows she is wrong. He runs up the tower with the hand-lamp and swings it on the gallery, side to side, the warning. Abery, on deck, sees "a light shown from the gallery, steady as I thought." At that distance a swung lamp is a smear.

**7.02 p.m.** Ottilie is on the gallery with a forty-second exposure of "the light in the gale" (**plate 8**). Penwarden bursts out beside her swinging the lamp. The plate records his fan of light, the white slot in the spray, and the ketch's masthead light running into it.

**7.15 p.m.** The *Annick Rose* strikes the Teeth.

**7.30 p.m.** All hands go to the landing. Penwarden gets a line across to the ketch on the second throw. Abery comes ashore along it, hand over hand.

**7.35 p.m.** Ottilie burns magnesium ribbon so the men can see, and opens her lens (**plate 9**). Davy kneels at the seaward edge of the iron landing stage with the boy Thomas Shilling by the wrist. Out by the outer rocks, Rype clings to the ketch's spanker boom. On the ketch, the mainmast goes over the wheel and Hosken.

**c. 7.40 p.m.** A sea comes over the stage. Davy heaves the boy up onto the iron and is carried off. He is never found.

**7.50 p.m.** Last of the ribbon (**plate 10**). The boy clings to the stanchion where Davy knelt. Davy's cap is caught on it. Penwarden crawls out to the boy. The boom is empty.

**c. 9.00 p.m.** Penwarden finds Rype's body in a gully below the landing, the brow cut open by the rocks. He carries it up.

**Saturday 12 Nov, 12.20 a.m.** In the cottage kitchen, Keturah has broached a cask of brandy that came ashore with the wreck and is dosing the cold men. Abery is bandaged, his arm in a sling. The boy sleeps on the settle. Penwarden sits with his head in his hands and his cup turned down. Rype lies on a door across two chairs under a sheet. Nell watches from the doorway. Ottilie's reflection shows in the black window (**plate 11**).

**c. 1.30 a.m.** Amos, on the lantern to see why the light "looked thin", feels the empty frame with his hands and finds the red tuft on the screw. He understands. He rigs canvas over the gap.

**2.58 a.m.** In the watch room Amos writes into the log, squeezed between the 19h00 and 19h15 lines: *"19h10 Sea broke in the lantern glazing & carried away Sector glass III. Canvas rigged. A.K."* Nell's scarf lies folded beside the log. Ottilie has followed him up and photographs him, deliberately (**plate 12**).

**c. 8.40 a.m.** Nell comes to Ottilie in the darkroom and asks her to keep the secret. Ottilie leaves the pane in her lamp ("I could not think of a darker place"), packs the exposed plates in the tin, and makes one last exposure of herself and the sleeping Nell. She leaves that plate in the camera (**plate 13**).

**10 a.m.** The relief boat. Ottilie leaves with the survivors. Her camera, lamp and tin stay in the darkroom, left in the Board's store "for collection". Nobody collects them.

**December 1887.** The Inquiry, at Trevennor, finds as printed in §7.3. Penwarden is dismissed.

**March 1888.** Penwarden is drowned. **May 1888.** Amos is retired for his sight. **30 August 1888.** Ottilie writes to the Board. **September 1888.** She dies. **16 October 1888.** The player arrives.

## 5. Staging conventions (for the tableau builder)

- **Units and camera.** Units are metres, with y up. Each set has its own frame; full set geometry is in GDD §6.2. The camera is Ottilie's stereoscopic camera: two parallel lenses **0.076 m** apart, with a **40° horizontal field of view** per eye unless the plate says otherwise. Each eye image is **480 × 424 px**.
- **"Zone"** is the tonal zone a clue lives in (GDD §5.6):
  - **shadow**: lost if the plate is *thin* (under-developed);
  - **highlight**: lost if the plate is *dense* (over-developed);
  - **mid**: survives any plate that is not fogged.
- **"Loupe ×n"** means the detail cannot be read without that loupe power.
- **"Eye R only"** means the detail is hidden behind a nearer object in the left image. It can only be seen in the right image (disocclusion).
- **Actinic values** are in GDD §6.6. They give how bright each thing photographs, from 0 to 1. Red things are about 0.04, so they photograph black.
- **Motion** marks what moved during the exposure. Moving things are accumulated over sub-frames and show as translucent ghosts. Still things are sharp.
- **Rebate markings.** Every plate carries the **pre-flashed 8-step wedge** in its bottom rebate. In the right half of the rebate, Ottilie's plate number is scratched into the emulsion in pencil-thin strokes 14 px high, such as `No. 7` or `TRIAL`.
- **Wick** is on every plate. Each plate gives Wick's position as a collectible hotspot (`kind: 'wick'`).

### Plate 8: "The Light in the Gale" (`p08`)
- **When:** Fri. 11th, 7.02 p.m. **Set:** `rock`, variant `nightGale`: rain, spray, a black sea.
- **Exposure:** **cap off, counted 40** (40 sec).
- **Composition:**
  - **Camera:** on the gallery, south-east side, looking east-south-east along the curve of the rail toward the Teeth.
  - **Penwarden, foreground right, at the rail.** **Motion:** his body sways, so he is a dim ghost. His **hand-lamp swings side to side at arm's length** across the whole exposure. On the plate this becomes a **bright fan of 9–11 overlapping arcs**, each about 70° wide, pivoting at his shoulder (highlight zone; any non-dense development).
  - **The light in the spray.** The station light lights the rain and spray around the tower. This is authored as a translucent cylinder of glow, radius 6–40 m (GDD §5.8):
    - **White sectors** glow bright.
    - **The red sector** (azimuth 70°–110°, toward the Teeth) **prints dark**.
    - **Inside the red sector, azimuth 86°–94°, there is a bright WHITE SLOT.** This is the beam through the empty opening III. It points straight at the Teeth (mid zone; obvious once noticed).
  - **The ketch.** Her **masthead light** leaves a short bright streak, about 25 px, **running into the white slot** (mid).

## 8. The Return: structure and answer key

The Return is the deduction book, with three pages to fill and a fourth for documents. GDD §3 and §4 cover the UX; this section is the truth table (`story/truth.ts`).

### 8.1 Persons (ten figures, confirmed in pairs)
A figure's row appears when its **portrait plate** is fixed. The portrait is cropped from the player's own development of that plate. Each row asks for a **Name** (one of the ten souls), a **Fate** (one of eight) and, for some fates, an **Agent** (a soul).

| Fig. | Portrait plate | Name | Fate | Agent |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| I | p01 | Saul Penwarden | Survived | — |
| II | p01 | Amos Ketteridge | Survived | — |
| III | p01 | Nell Ketteridge | Survived | — |
| IV | p01 | Keturah Ketteridge | Survived | — |
| V | p01 | David Lusk | Drowned saving… | Thomas Shilling |
| VI | p09 | Caleb Hosken | Crushed by the falling mast | — |
| VII | p09 | Anders Rype | Drowned | — |
| VIII | p11 | Joseph Abery | Rescued from the sea by… | Saul Penwarden |
| IX | p11 | Ottilie Frane | Survived | — |
| X | p10 | Thomas Shilling | Rescued from the sea by… | David Lusk |

**The eight fates (FINAL labels):** `Survived` · `Rescued from the sea by…` · `Drowned` · `Drowned saving…` · `Crushed by the falling mast` · `Struck down by…` · `Died of cold` · `Lost; body not found`.

**Rule:** whenever two or more rows are fully correct and not yet confirmed, the two earliest-edited are stamped **EXAMINED**, and so on in pairs. A single correct row waits for a partner. The game never says which rows are wrong.

### 8.2 Findings (five, confirmed one at a time, each with a cited plate)
Each Finding takes a verdict, **Stands** or **Withdraw**, plus one plate dragged from the plate strip. A Finding confirms only when the verdict is right *and* the cited plate is one of those listed below.

| Finding | Verdict | Valid citations | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Glass carried away at 7.10 p.m. | **Withdraw** | p05, p07, p12 | Opening III was already empty at 1.50 p.m. (p05) and at lighting-up (p07). The entry was written at 2.58 a.m. (p12). |
| 2. A steady light from the gallery | **Withdraw** | p08 | The trail is a swung fan of arcs, which is a warning, not a steady lure. |
| 3. Rype killed by a blow | **Withdraw** | p09, p10 | He was alone in the surf among the outer rocks, 22 m from anyone, then gone. The cut came from the rocks. |
| 4. Spirits broached, Penwarden answerable | **Withdraw** | p11 | Keturah pours for the cold men. Penwarden's cup is turned down. |
| 5. Lusk drowned while assisting the rescue | **Stands** | p09, p10 | It is true. It is there so that "withdraw everything" is never a safe guess. |

Stamp labels: **WITHDRAWN** or **STANDS**.

### 8.3 The Sector (four questions, confirmed together)
| Question (FINAL wording) | Options (FINAL) | Answer |
|---|---|---|
| **When was the glass of Sector III removed?** | Before 10.15 a.m. · Between 10.15 a.m. and 1.50 p.m. · Between 1.50 p.m. and lighting-up · At 7.10 p.m., by the sea | **Between 10.15 a.m. and 1.50 p.m.** |
| **Where is the glass now?** | *Cannot be chosen.* It is filled only by reading the pane on the light table, and is then stamped **FOUND: in the dark-room lamp**. | in the dark-room lamp |
| **By whose hand?** | any of the ten souls | **Nell Ketteridge** |
| **Why was the white not seen from the lantern?** | The watch was not kept · The Principal Keeper's sight had failed · Canvas was over the frame · The lamp was not yet lit | **The Principal Keeper's sight had failed** |

When all four are right (with Where filled by the physical act), the page is stamped **ACCOUNTED FOR**.

### 8.4 Sealing
- **Seal the Return** is available at any time.
  - If the Return is incomplete, it is sent back (ending C) and play continues.
  - If the Sector is **ACCOUNTED FOR**, sealing offers the final choice: **Name the hand** or **Write: "a person unknown"** (endings A and B).
- Persons and Findings do not need to be complete for A or B, but the Board's letter acknowledges how many were examined.

## 10. Red herrings (deliberate)

1. **Penwarden the wrecker.** The Inquiry's findings, his £14 debt (Exhibit 7), Abery's "steady light", his recovering Rype's body and the brandy all point at him. The plates clear him point by point. This is the misdirection the player starts with.
2. **"The red went white all of a sudden."** Abery's honest line seems to support the 7.10 p.m. story. It really describes the ketch sailing *into* the narrow white slot.
3. **Ottilie as the taker.** She needed red glass and had the motive, and players will suspect her. She was photographing elsewhere at 12.45 and 1.50. The scarf (C20, C39) and Nell's absence (C11) point away from her.
4. **Davy as careless youth.** He is on the landing at 1.50 (p05) and could have gone up the tower. But he is at dinner at 12.45 (p04), and the scarf is not his.
5. **"Struck down by…"** is offered as a fate because of Finding 3. No one was struck.
6. **The commended keeper.** The Inquiry praises Amos's "prompt entry of the Log". That entry is the lie.

## 11. Hints (Wick, SHOULD tier)

After 4 minutes without a new stamp, Wick (alive in 1888) walks along the bench and sits beside the rack slot of the plate that best serves the player's next missing deduction. Tapping Wick pencils one line into the Return's margin. These are the Examiner's own notes (FINAL):

1. *Davy would laugh. Which face would not keep still?*
2. *The muster says one keeper served in the Navy. Sailors mark their hands.*
3. *Red glass prints black. Count the black panes on every plate that shows the lantern.*
4. *Who was not at dinner?*
5. *The store had no spare ruby glass. Where did Miss Frane's come from?*
6. *Look at the lantern hatch with one eye, then the other.*
7. *A steady light leaves a point. What did the gallery lamp leave?*
8. *Abery came ashore on "the big man's" line.*
9. *Who stood nearest the Norwegian when the ribbon burned?*
10. *A cup turned down is a cup not drunk from.*
11. *What time does the watch-room clock say, and what time is he writing about?*
12. *"Let them develop the plates there, with my lamp."*

Hint 12 is the last resort. It is given only when everything except the Sector "Where" is confirmed and the player has not lifted the window within 3 minutes.

### 12.4 Ottilie's second letter (opens after either choice)
Envelope: *To be opened when the Return is made. O. F.*

> If you have read the lamp, you know.
>
> She was eleven. She saw that I could not work, and she loved the work, and she thought she could have the glass back in its frame before the light was wanted. I never asked her where she had found red glass the size of my window. I knew by supper. I did nothing until the ship was on the Teeth, and then there was nothing to do but burn ribbon.
>

## 14. Difficulty and pacing

| Phase | Plates | Minutes | What the player learns / feels | Deductions available |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Arrival** | TRIAL | 0–6 | Craft: rock, wedge, water, hypo, canary table, velvet, loupe, stereo. Wick. | none |
| **Bundle I** | 1–3 | 6–22 | A family, two keepers, a broken lamp. Cozy and curious. | Figures I–IV confirm easily as two pairs (Amos and Keturah; Nell and Penwarden). Davy's blur teaches the motion rule. |
| **Bundle II** | 4–7 | 22–42 | Unease. An empty chair, an empty frame, a lamp mended too neatly, a girl in the hatch. Sharp players suspect the lamp here. | Figure V. Sector "when". Suspicion of "who". |
| **Bundle III** | 8–12 | 42–68 | The storm, then grief. Davy's sacrifice. The lie being written. | Figures VI–X, all five Findings, Sector "who" and "why". |
| **The lamp** | — | 68–72 | The turn: you have been working under the evidence the whole time. | Sector "where" (FOUND) → ACCOUNTED FOR. |
| **Return and letters** | 13 | 72–85 | Mercy or candour; Ottilie's confession; plate 13; colour. | Ending A or B. |

**Fairness rules the content must keep:**
1. Every person can be named from at least two independent sources.
2. Every fate is shown on a plate, not only told in text.
3. Every Finding can be withdrawn from a plate that survives **Good** development.
4. No load-bearing clue sits *only* in a zone that a single mistake can destroy. Save pins and redevelopment make this safe; the shadow-zone clues (C17, C21, C27, C28) each have a mid-zone partner.
5. The answer to "where" is visible in the room from the first minute. The lamp has always been hanging there.

## 15. Originality notes

- **Shared with the genre:** fates confirmed in groups, a book of persons, a 1-bit look, and deduction from frozen moments.
- **Our own:**
  - the darkroom (development quality decides what you can see);
  - the photographic-physics clue grammar: blue-blindness, long-exposure ghosting, stereo disocclusion and light trails;
  - the setting, story, cast and documents;
  - the citation rule for Findings;
  - the mercy choice;
  - the physical climax at the lamp.
- **Names checked against the other games in this collection:** no Lyle, Wren, Penhallow (Latent), and no "O." (Darkroom's previous keyholder is a different person: our photographer is always written "Miss Frane" or "O. F.", never "O.").
