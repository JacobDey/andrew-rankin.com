# Plates: concept through the CRAFT, AUTHENTICITY & VIBE lens

*Game 3, slug `plates`. The darkroom game styled after the Obra Dinn look: 1-bit, period setting, a deduction mystery. Everything below is original.*

**Title:** **THE CAUL ROCK PLATES**
**Subtitle:** *an inquest in silver, 1879*
(Alternates: "Non-Actinic", "Twelve Plates from a Dark Light". The hub card reads: *"A lighthouse went dark for nine nights. Twelve undeveloped plates know why."*)

---

## 0. Thesis: 1-bit is the physics, not a filter

A silver-halide grain is a binary device. After development each crystal is either metallic silver (black) or it is not there at all. Tone is only the *density* of black grains. So in this game the dither is not a post effect laid over a grey image. **The dither is the emulsion.** Each plate is a fixed field of about 900k simulated grains with a seeded position and sensitivity. Exposure sets each grain's probability of being developable, and development flips grains from cream to black one at a time. That has three consequences:

1. The dither never swims, because the grains are stuck to the glass.
2. Watching a plate develop means watching the 1-bit image fill in grain by grain. This is the signature effect.
3. Every look decision ties back to something a photographer would recognize. The marketing line is *"Every pixel is a silver grain."*

Visual grammar: **the present** (your darkroom, the tower) uses *ordered* Bayer dither with engraved edge lines, like a print or a Board circular. **The past** (anything inside a plate) uses *stochastic* blue-noise grain. At a glance you always know whether you are looking at the world or at a photograph of it.

## 1. Fiction

### Setting
**Caul Rock Light**: a granite tower on a bare stack 14 miles offshore, with a first-order revolving light showing *three flashes every 20 seconds*. It is November 1879. Gelatin dry plates have just reached amateurs, and unlike wet collodion they **keep a latent image for months**. That one fact is the premise. *(A caul is the birth membrane sailors once bought as a charm against drowning. The rock is named for its shape and its irony.)*

### Premise
The light went dark for nine nights during a gale. When the relief boat landed it found two dead, three missing, and three survivors barricaded in the oil store. The survivors were a woman who will not speak, her daughter, and a foreign seaman nobody can name. The woman, **Hester Marr**, is an amateur photographer. Her plate box holds **twelve exposed, undeveloped quarter-plates** in six double dark slides, and her pocket *Register of Exposures* gives lens stop and count for each one.

**You** are a plate-developer engaged by the *Commissioners of Lights* (an invented body) to sit inquest by chemistry. You set up a ruby-lit darkroom in the round store-room at the tower's base. Fill in the Commissioners' **Form L.17, "Return of Persons upon the Station"**: name, station rating, plates seen in, disposition, and agency.

### The nine souls
| # | Name | Rating | Fate (solution) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Elias Brede | Principal Keeper, 58 | Poisoned, by accident (cyanide in a ginger-beer bottle) |
| 2 | Tobias Marr | 1st Assistant | Fell from the gallery while fighting Ashdown |
| 3 | Noll Fenwick | 2nd Assistant, 22 | Drowned rowing the blue gig for the mainland |
| 4 | Hester Marr | Tobias's wife, the photographer | Living |
| 5 | Pim Marr | Their daughter, 9 | Living. She took plate 10 herself |
| 6 | Clement Ashdown | Board engineer, there to inspect the new clockwork | Struck by Tobias, died at the stair foot |
| 7 | Daniel Rooke | Tender boatman, stranded | Drowned with Fenwick |
| 8 | "The man from the water" | Survivor of a wrecked brig | Living. His name and home port are read from the knit of his gansey |
| 9 | Sauce | The station cat | Living, and in five plates as a red herring and a scale reference |

### The truth, as a chain (accident, then suspicion, then cascade)
Hester decanted her **potassium cyanide** into an old stoneware ginger-beer bottle. She used it to clear silver-nitrate stains from her fingers, which was common, lethal practice at the time. Rain washed off the pencil label. On night 4 Brede, who had a cold, drank from it in the dark and died sitting at his desk in the watch room. Twenty minutes later Hester took a 40-second lamplight exposure of the watch room to show Ashdown the beam. **In plate 7 Brede is the sharpest thing in the frame because he is already dead.** Ashdown smelled bitter almonds. Tobias could not (it's genetic; see §3). Tobias suspected the stranger and then Ashdown. Fenwick had used the bottle and knew whose it was. He fled with Rooke to fetch a coroner and drowned. On night 6 Tobias attacked Ashdown while Ashdown was winding the clockwork. Both died, and **the lens stopped turning mid-exposure in plate 11.**

### Three textual voices (and no recorded dialogue)
- **The Commissioners**: printed circular prose in small caps, cold and procedural. *"Developers will on no account admit white light to the store."*
- **Hester's Register**: pencil, terse, numbered. *"No. 7. Watch room, lamp lit. Stop f/11, cap off, counted 40. Mr A. would not keep still. Mr B. dozing, a mercy."* These lines read differently once you know the truth, and every one should be written to do that.
- **Your bench notes**: the player's own margin annotations, auto-filled when you discover something (*"Fenwick's fingers black on the 13th, clean by the 15th"*).

## 2. Photographic authenticity: the process, with numbers

Each step is a hands-on action. No step is a button.

| Step | What you do | Real basis |
|---|---|---|
| Unload | Slide the plate out of the double dark slide by ruby light, then check the notch to find the emulsion side | Dry plates are blue-sensitive, so **ruby glass (passes ~600 nm and up) is safe**. Amber light fogs slowly, and white light ruins a plate instantly |
| Develop | Pour **pyro-ammonia** in *one sweep* from the tray's near edge, then rock it | A hesitant pour leaves *developer lines*. Typical development runs 2-4 minutes at about 18 °C |
| Brush | Touch out **air bells** with a camel-hair brush | Trapped bubbles leave clear round dots where no development happened |
| Judge | Lift the plate and look **through the back** under the ruby lamp. When the image shows from the back, stop | This is "development by inspection". There is no timer, only the eye and your pocket watch ticking |
| Restrain | Add drops of **potassium bromide** to a fogged plate | Bromide slows development, and fog more than image. This is the plate-11 rescue |
| Fix | Move it to **hypo** (sodium thiosulfate, about 1 part to 4 of water). The creamy silver bromide clears from the back like mist burning off | The rule of thumb: fix for twice the clearing time |
| Wash | Rainwater from the cistern, in a rocking trough | Fresh water is scarce on a rock, so you get a fixed water budget (a gentle resource, not a fail state) |
| Dry and varnish | Optional spirit bath to dry fast. Sandarac varnish warmed over the lamp | Alcohol dehydration was a period shortcut |
| Rescue | **Intensify** (mercuric chloride) a thin plate, or **reduce** (potassium ferricyanide plus hypo) a dense one | Both are real techniques. Ferricyanide is the same compound as cyanotype's part B, which links to Game 2 and its "cyanide chemistry" |

**Plate physics** (numbers for the shader):
- Quarter-plate is **3¼ × 4¼ in (83 × 108 mm)** on 2 mm glass. Grain field is 960 × 744, about 8.9 px/mm.
- **Spectral response:** sensitivity is roughly the scene's blue channel. `lum = 0.05R + 0.15G + 0.80B`, plus a UV bonus on sky. Red paint photographs black. Blue paint and blue sky photograph white, which is why period skies are blank.
- **Characteristic curve:** an S-curve with a toe and a shoulder (`D = Dmax / (1 + exp(-k(logE − logE0)))`). Gross overexposure **solarizes**, meaning density falls again, so the burning lamp in a night plate prints as a *black sun*.
- **Halation:** light bounces inside the glass at the critical angle (41.8° for n = 1.5). That makes a ring of radius ≈ 2·t·tan θc ≈ **3.6 mm ≈ 32 px** around every point highlight. Old plates had no anti-halation backing, so every lamp and every sunlit wave crest gets a ghost ring.
- **Lens:** Hester owns a *rapid rectilinear f/8* (sharp, flat field) and a stereo pair with lenses 3 in apart. Stops are **slot-in brass plates**. The shutter is **the lens cap**: "cap off, count 40". Apply cos⁴θ vignetting at the corners.
- **Exposure:** early gelatin is roughly ISO 5-10 in modern terms, about 10× collodion. A grey Atlantic noon at f/16 takes 1-2 s. A window-lit interior at f/8 takes 15-30 s. Lamplight takes 40 s or more. **Long exposures create the deduction grammar below.**
- **Edge truths:** the dark-slide rebate leaves a clean unexposed margin. Pencil holder numbers ("3A", "3B") are scratched on the slides. A partial light leak gives a *fog wedge* from one edge.

### The clue grammar: physics is the witness
Every clue type is a real optical or chemical fact. The game teaches each one once with a tutorial plate, then trusts you.

1. **Stillness = sharpness.** The image integrates across the exposure: render N sub-frames of the animated tableau and accumulate them. Movers become translucent ghosts and the motionless are crisp. (The Victorian corpse-on-a-stand story is mostly myth. This is the physics underneath it.)
2. **Blue-blindness.** Everyone swears "the *red* boat left at dawn". In Pim's plate 10 the boat is *white*, so it was the station's blue gig, not the brig's red boat.
3. **The beam as a clock.** In night plates the revolving lens leaves bright bars on the fog. Three bars per 20 s, so counting bars gives the exposure length. In plate 11 the bars *stop half-formed*: that is the instant the clockwork lost its winder.
4. **The weight trunk.** The clockwork weight drops down a tube in the tower wall and is wound every ~3 h. Where a plate shows it, its height dates the moment.
5. **Stained hands.** Silver nitrate turns skin black. Fenwick's fingertips are black in plate 2 and clean in plate 5, so he used the cyanide to clean them.
6. **Viewpoint height.** The horizon sits low in the frame and the camera looks up at the rail. A 4-ft photographer took plate 10.
7. **Fog wedge.** Someone pulled slide 6's shutter halfway in daylight to destroy plate 11. Half the plate is black fog and half is the key image, and that half is only recoverable with bromide restrainer.
8. **Ganseys.** Knit patterns (cables, moss, "marriage lines") were famously specific to particular harbours. In 1-bit grain the stitches read clearly. Match the stranger's pattern to the home-port pattern card in the Commissioners' packet.

## 3. "A photographer would smile" details

- The plate box label: *"VELLACOTT BROS. GELATINO-BROMIDE DRY PLATES, Extra Rapid, 12 Quarter-Plate. To be opened only in non-actinic light."* (That is the real period phrase.)
- **Poison bottles are ribbed** so you can tell them by touch in the dark. That was real practice from the 1870s on. The fatal bottle was *smooth stoneware*, which is the whole tragedy in one tactile fact. On phones, `navigator.vibrate` gives the ribbed bottle a buzzing pattern and the smooth bottle silence.
- **Bitter almonds:** a sizeable minority of people (often cited as 1 in 5 to 2 in 5) genetically cannot smell cyanide. In Hester's Register: *"Mr A. says the tea smells of almonds; T. smells nothing; men will argue over anything."*
- Your view-model hands **stain** with pyro and silver over the session and never wash clean.
- The **gansey card**, the **Form L.17**, and a **cyanotype blueprint of the tower** (white lines on Prussian blue, period-correct since commercial blueprint paper dates from the 1870s) are the only non-photo documents. The blueprint is the map and the one blue thing in the game.

## 4. Art direction

### Render pipeline (per frame)
1. Render the 3D scene at an **internal 800 × 450** (phones: 640 × 360), then integer-upscale with nearest-neighbour filtering.
2. Pass A, **spectral luminance**: each material carries an RGB albedo, and the plate camera computes the blue-weighted value from §2. The present-day view uses ordinary luminance.
3. Pass B, **edges**: Sobel on depth and normals gives 1 px ink lines, but only on silhouettes and creases with an angle over 35°. The look should be engraving, not comic book.
4. Pass C, **threshold**: the present uses an 8×8 Bayer matrix anchored to *world-space triplanar UVs* on large surfaces, which stops swimming during camera tilt. Plates use the seeded grain field from §0.
5. Palette map: two colours, never more.

### Palettes ("Print Process" setting; each defaults by context)
| Name | Ink | Light | Used for |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Ruby** | `#160505` | `#C9361F` | The darkroom under the safelight (default in-room) |
| **Glass** | `#0B0C0C` | `#CDD5D1` | A negative held against the porthole |
| **Ambrotype** | `#0A0908` | `#BDB59A` | The positive over black velvet |
| **Albumen** | `#23160D` | `#EDDDB9` | Finished prints in the Form, the hub card, the title |
| **Prussian** | `#13396A` | `#E8EEF2` | The blueprint map only |
| **Lamp** | `#1A1206` | `#F2B654` | Lantern-room plates at night |

Transitions are hard cuts with a three-frame Bayer cross-dissolve, never colour fades. Opening the darkroom door with a plate out flashes the screen to pure Light for 6 frames. The ruined plate then develops to solid Ink: a one-second tragedy you will not repeat.

### Typography
Bundle the **IM Fell** family (digitized 17th-century Fell types, OFL, via `@fontsource/im-fell-french-canon` and `@fontsource/im-fell-great-primer`, no CDN). Use French Canon for titles and the Form and Great Primer for body text. Draw all in-world text to canvas and threshold it, so type is also 1-bit and slightly ink-spread. Hester's hand uses a **procedural pencil**: stroke paths with jittered width and a grain texture. Small labels use a hand-defined **5×7 bitmap font** stored as a string array in code. Numbers use old-style figures. The Form uses ruled lines, dotted leaders and a printer's imprint line: *"L.17, Printed for the Commissioners, 1876."*

### Materials and lighting
- **Darkroom:** a round granite room (the curved bench follows the wall), whitewashed lime, a slate sink, a deal-wood bench, stoneware jars, ribbed glass bottles, and a porthole glazed with ruby glass. There is **one light**: the ruby lantern, an oil flame with 1/f flicker at ±6% intensity. Every 20 seconds the tower's beam passes overhead and **brightens the porthole in a 3-flash pattern**. It is your metronome and your ghost.
- **Plate tableaux:** low-poly granite, iron, brass and oilskin, lit by a single overcast skylight for day plates and the lamp for night plates. In the stereoscope you get **parallax limited to the 3-in stereo baseline**: lean with mouse, gyro or touch, about ±4° and a few centimetres. That is just enough depth to separate the bottle on the desk from the one on the shelf behind it.

## 5. Procedural audio (WebAudio, no samples)

- **Sea:** brown noise into a 300 Hz lowpass, swelling on a 9-13 s randomized LFO. Wave strikes on the tower are a 45 Hz sine thump with a 1.5 s decay, plus a bandpassed noise burst and a delayed stone **creak** (narrow 180 Hz resonance with stick-slip jitter).
- **The clockwork overhead:** an escapement tick-tock (2.4 kHz and 2.1 kHz clicks, 1 s period) and the regulator fan's whirr. The weight-cable creak gets *slower as the weight nears the bottom*. In plate-11 flashbacks on the Form, the tick **stops**.
- **Fog bell:** inharmonic partials (1, 2.76, 5.40, 8.93 × 220 Hz) every 20 s when there's fog. Ring it on the title screen.
- **Liquids:** tray slosh is bandpassed noise whose centre frequency and gain follow the simulated fluid's surface velocity. Pours are bubble "glugs" synthesized at the **Minnaert frequency, f ≈ 3.26/r** (a 3 mm bubble is about 1.1 kHz), with pitch rising as the bottle's Helmholtz air volume shrinks.
- **Small sounds:** glass set down is modal synthesis (4-5 inharmonic partials, 80 ms); the dark slide is a stick-slip wooden scrape; hypo crystals crunch granularly; a 4 Hz pocket-watch tick is always audible, and it is how you count.
- **The fixing chord:** when a plate fully clears in hypo, a **glass-harmonica** swell plays (sines with slow attack, 0.5-2 Hz beating). Each of the twelve plates has its own chord. The full Return, once complete, plays all twelve in order as the game's only piece of music. Apart from these chords there is no score: just the sea, the clock and your hands.

## 6. Tactile and UI touches

- **No HUD.** The Form lies on the bench, the Register hangs on a nail, and plates rest in a drying rack where each slot holds one finished plate.
- **The velvet flip** (the real ambrotype trick: a thin negative over black reads as a positive): drag the black velvet square behind a plate and the image inverts from negative to positive in a grain-by-grain wipe that follows the cloth's edge.
- **Loupe:** a brass 8× loupe that shows real grain clumping at the plate's native resolution. It is the only way to read the gansey stitches and the bottle's missing label.
- **Saves:** autosave to IndexedDB after every fixed plate. Undeveloped plates are one-shot, which is the period truth, but the rescue chemistry means no plate is ever unreadable, only harder.
- **Anti-guessing:** Form rows are certified two at a time. A correct pair earns a stamp: *"EXAMINED: C. of L."*
- **`window.__game`:** `developPlate(n, quality)`, `setPalette(name)`, `viewPlate(n, 'neg'|'pos'|'stereo')`, `fillRow(i, {...})`, `seed`. This gives Playwright deterministic screenshots of every palette and plate.

## 7. Three screenshot moments

1. **The grain bloom (plate 1, the station portrait).** You rock the tray under ruby light and black grains sparkle onto cream: the lamp and sky first, then faces. Nine people assemble out of noise while the porthole pulses three times. Lift the plate, fix it, watch the milk clear, slide the velvet behind it, and **nine faces flip to positive** under your fingertip. The thumbnail is a ruby-and-black half-negative, half-positive plate with the velvet's edge cutting it in two.
2. **The dead man is the sharpest thing in the room (plate 7).** In the stereoscope, Ashdown is a translucent smear crossing the watch room, the beam has left two pale bars on the window fog, and Brede sits at his desk **pin-sharp, eyes open**. Lean in on the parallax and the ginger-beer bottle separates from the shadow of the lamp. Players will post it with "wait."
3. **The black sun (plate 11).** A night exposure from the gallery: the lamp solarized to a **black disc with a halation ring**, a fan of beam bars sweeping the fog, and the last bar **cut off half-formed**. Half the plate is a fog wedge you saved with bromide drop by drop. In the clear half, two figures are at the rail. Lamp palette, amber and black. This is the poster.

## 8. Build notes and cuts for this lens

- **Must-have:** the grain-field shader (the whole identity), sub-frame exposure accumulation, spectral luminance, ruby darkroom with the beam pulse, velvet flip, 12 plate tableaux from **one** shared low-poly tower kit (watch room, gallery, stair, store, landing stage), the Form, and the sea, clock and glug audio.
- **Nice-to-have:** stereoscope parallax, halation and solarization, haptic bottles, procedural pencil.
- **Cut first:** varnishing, the water budget, the Lamp palette (fall back to Glass).
- **Phones / no WebGL2:** development is one cheap fragment shader (`grainThreshold < developProgress × exposure`). Without WebGL2, fall back to a Canvas2D viewer of pre-dithered plates; the mystery still works.
