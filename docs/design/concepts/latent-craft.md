# LATENT IMAGE: The Kessane Hold

**Subtitle:** *Eleven rolls in a police cold store, twenty-four years undeveloped. Develop them. Look closely at who was holding the camera.*
**Hub card line:** *A police story told in silver.*

Concept by the **craft, authenticity and vibe** lens. Slug `latent`. It shares the 1-bit DNA of `plates` but keeps its own era, materials, sound and palette. Every name, place, institution and line of text here is original.

"Latent" means two things. A **latent image** is exposed film that hasn't been developed yet. A **latent print** is a fingerprint nobody can see until it is developed. In this game both are true at once.

---

## 1. The fiction

### Frame: one night, 28 March 2003
Varrow is a fictional, rain-heavy river city. Its police **Photographic Unit** shuts down tomorrow, and the department goes digital on Monday. You are a records contractor on the night shift with a work order glowing on an amber terminal:

> WORK ORDER 03-0417. PURGE PHOTOGRAPHIC UNIT COLD STORE, SHELF 4. LATENT (UNDEVELOPED) MATERIAL TO BE PROCESSED AND INDEXED PER REG. 14.2 PRIOR TO DISPOSAL. REQUESTED BY: R. KESSANE, RECORDS (CONTRACT).

Shelf 4 holds one carton marked **HOLD — R.K.** The property log says 10 rolls. There are **11**.

The night has no timer. A wall clock runs from 21:00 to 06:00, and each process step advances it. At dawn a grey line shows under the darkroom door. Nothing in the game punishes slowness.

### Cast
| Name | 1979 | Detail the player can verify |
|---|---|---|
| **Ruth Kessane** | Unit photographer, badge P-114, 38 | 1.80 m tall. Always shoots a **slate frame** first (case number chalked on a board). Brackets ±1 stop. Writes labels in **block capitals**. White tape on her camera strap. |
| **Mina Lusk** | Night printer operator at Sallow Valley Photofinishing, 26 | 1.60 m tall (on her employee card). Writes in **looping cursive**. Ruth's friend. Never stated anywhere, but the photographs make it plain they were more than that. |
| **DS Conrad Hale** | Fire & Insurance Squad | In 2003, Deputy Chief, with a retirement dinner on 4 April. His boot heel has a diagonal cut. |
| **Lyle Dunmore** | Owner of the photofinishing plant, which was failing | Heavily insured. |
| **PC Bo Atwell** | First officer on scene | In 2003, a sergeant. His statement is honest and overlooked. |
| **Dot Penhallow** | Unit clerk | Her margin notes are warm, nosy and accurate. She is the game's chorus. |
| **Peter Odell** | The Unit's other photographer | He shot the *official* fire-scene photos, which are already in the database. |

### The truth (never stated outright; the player assembles it)
1. **Dec 1978.** Hale runs an insurance-arson racket for failing businesses. A birdwatcher's roll of herons, sent to Sallow Valley for printing, catches Hale's car and two fuel cans behind the Tannery two hours before it burned.
2. **15 Feb 1979.** Mina spots it while checking prints and calls Ruth. Ruth comes to the plant after midnight and copies the prints on Mina's light table (Roll 7).
3. **16 Feb, 02:40.** Dunmore has told Hale, who sets the plant alight: one fire collects the insurance and destroys the evidence. **Ruth dies inside. Mina gets out** through the loading dock with Ruth's camera bag, shooting the last frames of Roll 8 as she runs.
4. The body is "identified" as Mina from her engraved watch and her timecard, which shows IN 22:00 and no OUT. Hale signs off that no dental comparison is needed.
5. Mina **becomes Ruth**. She phones in sick in Ruth's name ("very hoarse"), posts the HOLD carton to the Unit, and mails a resignation letter. She spends two weeks photographing Hale and Dunmore (Rolls 9–10) before she loses her nerve and leaves Varrow.
6. **1987.** She posts an **11th roll**: one frame of the same window on the 16th of every month for three years, a memorial. Dot logs it into the HOLD carton.
7. **2003.** Hale is about to retire with honours, so "R. Kessane" files a purge work order. Reg. 14.2 forces someone neutral to develop the rolls first. She can't make her own accusation, because her own identity is part of the crime. She needs a witness, and that witness is you.

### The eleven rolls
| Roll | Content | Craft hook |
|---|---|---|
| 1–4 | Ruth's routine scenes from Jan–Feb 1979: a burglary, a collision, the Tannery aftermath | Teach the slate habit, her eye height and her camera's **gate notch**. Tannery frames show soot footprints only under **oblique light**. |
| 5 | Unit Christmas party, Dec 1978 | Ruth is the tall one at the back, with the white-taped strap. |
| 6 | Ruth's personal roll: Mina at the plant, at a diner, asleep on a bus | The tender heart of the story. It never makes it into the documents. |
| 7 | Copy-stand frames of the heron prints | Hale's plate is legible only if you **burn in** the background on a hard filter. |
| 8 | The fire night | Starts with Ruth and a slate. Mid-roll, three frames are blown out by flame **halation**. The last frames have no slate, a lower horizon and motion blur. |
| 9–10 | Night surveillance of Hale and Dunmore | **Pushed two stops**. The cassette is labelled "+2" in cursive. Develop it at normal time and the evidence sits in the lost shadows. |
| 11 | Memorial window roll. Cassette carries the 1983 checkerboard code. | Proves the carton was opened after 1979. Frame 36's window reflection shows who is behind the camera. |

### The clue web: every load-bearing fact appears twice
- **Mina outlived the fire.** (a) The coroner estimates stature at 175–180 cm from femur length, but Mina's employee card says 5'3". (b) Post-fire frames are shot from a camera 2–3 brick courses lower (§3, Brick Course).
- **Ruth didn't phone in sick.** (a) Dot's day book: "Very hoarse. Said not to send anyone round." (b) The label on the HOLD lid is in cursive. Dot's own note says "Odd, Ruth always prints her labels."
- **Same camera, different hands.** All eleven rolls show the same gate notch, so the camera survived. Slate habit and eye height change mid-Roll 8.
- **Hale was there.** (a) The cut heel shows in the soot footprints at the Tannery and again at the plant's dock. (b) His plate appears in the heron copy (Roll 7) and in the Roll 8 dock frames.

### Endings (Barlow-style: the game offers no verdict, only a choice)
Once the player has seen Roll 11 frame 36, the terminal accepts `CLOSE 03-0417` with two dispositions:
- **FILE**: the carton goes to Internal Affairs. A final clipping reads "Deputy Chief's retirement postponed." Then one terminal line: `CONTRACTOR R. KESSANE: NO SUCH EMPLOYEE`.
- **PURGE**: the silver-recovery unit hums, and the clock reaches 06:00. One print stays on the drying line. The player chooses which.

### Database voice: sample fragments (original)
> **DAY BOOK 19.2.79** R.K. phoned in sick 07:10. Very hoarse. Said not to send anyone round. Have put her tea mug in the cupboard. D.P.

> **PROPERTY 79-0311** Recd by post 1 carton, 10 × 35mm cassettes, exposed. Lid marked HOLD — R.K. Shelf 4, cold store (4 °C). *Odd, Ruth always prints her labels.* D.P.

> **STATEMENT, PC ATWELL 16.2.79** Someone small went off the end of the dock toward the river path. I took them for a kid. Nobody asked me again.

> **ID FORM 79-F-022** Deceased: LUSK, M. Identified by effects (wristwatch engraved "M."; timecard). Dental comparison: NOT REQUIRED. C. Hale DS.

> **PROPERTY 87-0040** 1 further cassette recd by post, no note, added to HOLD R.K. I haven't told anyone. Who would I tell. D.P.

The tone is quiet, procedural and rainy. Reports are terse police prose. People slip only in the margins.

## 2. Art direction

### Principle: every medium is 1-bit in its own way
Only the medium changes the dither, so a player can tell what a record *is* before reading it. That is a visual grammar `plates` does not have.

| Medium | 1-bit signature |
|---|---|
| **Silver negatives and prints** | A **grain-seeded threshold dither** anchored in *film space*: 2–3 octaves of clumped noise per roll. Zooming in or enlarging scales the grain the way a real enlarger does. Push-processed rolls have visibly coarser clumps. Grain is never screen-space, so it never swims. |
| **Newspaper clippings** | Round halftone dots at 85 lpi and 45°. |
| **Typed reports** | System `Courier New` drawn to canvas with per-glyph baseline jitter of ±0.6 px, uneven ribbon ink, and filled-in `e`/`o` counters. The Bureau typewriter's **lowercase r drops 1 px**. Hale's reports and the anonymous Tannery tip-off share it, so the tip came from inside the station. |
| **2003 faxes** | Thermal-paper banding with grey fade at the edges. |
| **Terminal** | Phosphor. See below. |

### Palettes: three lights, one spot colour
| Space | Ink / paper | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Terminal ("P3 amber") | `#1A0E00` / `#FFB000` | Bloom and phosphor persistence. |
| Film loading ("Total dark") | `#000000` | The only light is the timer's luminous hands, `#7DFF9A` at 6%. |
| Printing ("Safelight OC") | `#120503` / `#D2692A` | Amber safelight, around 589 nm. |
| White-light inspection ("Fibre") | `#0B0B0C` / `#F1EDE3` | Fibre paper: deep Dmax, warm base. |
| Optional ("RC neutral", "Selenium") | | Unlockable paper stocks. |

**The grease-pencil rule.** The only non-palette colour in the game is **red china-marker wax, `#C4202B`**. It marks everything *the player* decides: circled frames, crop marks, measured horizons, underlined search terms, and string on the corkboard. On the amber terminal it becomes inverse video. *The world is silver. Your judgement is red.*

**The sodium tie-in.** Varrow's 1979 streets are lit by low-pressure sodium lamps, which are almost purely 589 nm. Night scenes outside are therefore literally safelight-coloured. The city was already monochrome. A photographer will grin at that.

### Typography (all local, no font downloads)
- **Terminal:** an original **8×14 bitmap font** stored as a hex string, about 1.4 KB for 95 glyphs. It has VT-style double-height headers.
- **Handwriting:** an original **single-stroke vector alphabet**, in the spirit of 1960s plotter fonts, drawn with a waxy brush. The brush varies pressure, breaks on "paper tooth" noise and leaves wax build-up at stroke ends. Two hands from one skeleton: **Ruth = upright capitals; Mina = slanted, looped cursive**. Handwriting is a clue, so both must read as different at a glance.
- **UI chrome:** none of its own. Labels sit on objects as Dymo-style embossed tape, painter's tape and cassette stickers.

### Materials and lighting (Three.js scenes, kept small)
- **Desk:** a CRT with curved glass, rendered via a render target onto a bulged mesh. Faint reflections of the room sit on the glass. ±2° parallax follows the pointer.
- **Darkroom:** a stainless sink, three trays (developer, stop, fix), the enlarger column, the timer and the drying line. The single safelight is a point light with soft falloff.
- **Liquids:** a heightfield slosh, the safelight's specular on the meniscus, and refraction of the print below. Developer is a hair warmer in tone than fixer. The **development front** spreads from wherever the liquid first touches the paper, so a print slid in unevenly develops unevenly, as it would in life.
- **Post-processing:** CRT barrel (k = 0.08), 2 px scanlines, aperture grille and persistence (0.82 decay per frame) on the terminal only. The darkroom gets a **dark-adaptation ramp**: on entry the exposure starts at −2 EV and opens over about 20 s, the way eyes adjust. Flipping the white light blows out to full white for 400 ms. If paper was out, it is **fogged**, and the game shows it.
- **Fallback without WebGL2:** every surface also exists as a 2D-canvas path. The dither and the grease pencil are canvas-native anyway.

## 3. Photographic authenticity: real steps, real numbers

### Film: "Varrow Pan 400" (fictional stock, real behaviour)
| Step | Real practice the game simulates |
|---|---|
| Open the cassette | Pop the cap with a **bottle opener**, in total darkness. |
| Square the leader | Scissors snip. |
| Load the stainless reel | **By feel and sound**. A kink leaves **crescent marks** on the negative. |
| Developer | Classic metol–hydroquinone–borax formula (metol 2 g, hydroquinone 5 g, sulfite 100 g, borax 2 g per litre). Stock: **8:00 at 20 °C**. |
| Temperature | **The heating is off tonight** and the lab sits at 15 °C, so the player builds a water bath. Drift costs about **9% of time per °C**, shown on a time/temperature chart taped to the wall. |
| Agitation | 30 s continuous, then **4 inversions every 30 s**. **Rap the tank on the counter** to free air bubbles. Skip that and pinhole bubble marks print as black dots. Over-agitate and **surge marks** stream from the sprocket holes. |
| Pushed rolls (9–10) | Marked "+2" (EI 1600): **+60% time**. Develop them normally and the evidence sits in the shadows you lost. |
| 24-year-old latent image | The log shows Roll 8 sat in a **car glovebox** for two weeks before it was posted. It needs **1% benzotriazole** (about 15 ml per litre) to hold back fog, plus +25% time. Other rolls are cold-stored and only lightly base-fogged. |
| Stop | 2% acetic acid, 30 s. A vinegar line in the flavour text. |
| Fix | **Clip test**: drop a leader trimming in the fixer, time how long it takes to clear, then fix for **twice that**. Under-fixed film shows as a milky dither haze. |
| Hypo clear, wash | Hypo clear 2 min. Wash with the 5/10/20 inversion method. |
| Wetting agent, dry | Wetting agent 1:200. Hang to dry with a weighted clip. Squeegee too hard and you get a long white scratch through six frames. |

**Edge print.** Frame numbers run `1 → 1A → 2`. A two-glyph date code repeats along the rebate. The cassette checkerboard (the 1983 auto-sensing code) dates Roll 11 as impossible for 1979.

### Printing
- **Contact sheet:** strips of six under glass. Read it with an 8× loupe and mark it in red.
- **Enlarger:** 50 mm f/2.8 lens (format diagonal ≈ 43 mm), stopped to **f/8**. Focus with a **grain focuser**: the clumps visibly snap sharp.
- **Test strip:** 4-second steps. A f-stop timing toggle (⅓-stop steps, ×1.26) is there for nerds.
- **Contrast filters 0–5.** **Dodge** the shadows to reveal a face in a doorway. **Burn** the highlights to pull a plate out of a window glare.
- **Print developer:** resin-coated paper 60 s, fibre 2–3 min. Prints *develop to completion*, so the game is lenient here (unlike film) until safelight fog.
- **Dry-down:** prints look about 8% lighter wet under safelight than they dry. The white-light view shows the truth.
- **Coin test:** an optional safelight check. A coin left on paper for 5 minutes shows a ghost outline if the safelight is leaking.

### Forensic photography of 1979, not 2003
Overall, mid-range and close-up shots. A **plain 15 cm steel rule** for scale, since L-scales with grey circles came later. Off-camera flash on a PC cord at **10–15° grazing** for soot footprints. Flash guide number 32 m at ISO 100, so **GN 64 at ISO 400: f/8 reaches 8 m**. Copy-stand work uses two lamps at 45°.

### "Brick Course": the deduction a photographer will love
In any level photograph, **the horizon cuts every vertical surface at the camera's height**. Varrow brick runs **75 mm per course** (65 mm brick + 10 mm joint).

The player draws the horizon in red through two vanishing points, and the game counts the courses below it:
- **Ruth:** 1.80 m tall, camera at about 1.66 m, so **22 courses**.
- **Post-fire photographer:** about 1.48 m, so **19–20 courses**.

The same alley appears in Roll 3 and in Roll 9. Two red lines, three bricks apart.

## 4. Procedural audio (WebAudio only)
| Source | Synthesis |
|---|---|
| Keyclick | 4 ms noise burst through a 3.2 kHz bandpass (Q 2), plus a 140 Hz sine thunk with 30 ms decay. ±8% pitch; the key-up is softer. |
| CRT | **15,734 Hz flyback whine** at −48 dB, with a toggle labelled "for the young". Boot **degauss**: a 60 Hz thoonk plus a 1.2 s noise swell. |
| Query | Hard-disk seek: 3–7 impulse ticks through a 1.8 kHz resonator, 8–20 ms apart. |
| Printout | 9-pin dot-matrix: about 1.2 kHz impact buzz, line feeds and a falling sweep on the carriage return. |
| Film loading | Pull "zip": bandpassed noise whose centre tracks pull velocity. A reel **tick** on each spiral turn. **Crinkle** (three crackles) on a kink. On Android, `navigator.vibrate` pulses for the ticks and the kink. |
| Tank | Inversion **glug**: two resonators that slide with the air volume. **Rap**: a 300 Hz thud plus a 2.8 kHz stainless ring. Lid: clunk. |
| Mechanical timer | Two-part escapement tick-tock at 1 Hz. Bell partials at 1,820 and 4,470 Hz, 1.5 s decay. |
| Enlarger | 60 Hz hum with 120/180 Hz harmonics, a fan, and a relay clack on the timer. |
| Room | 40 Hz HVAC rumble. The cold-store compressor kicks in about every 4 minutes (relay plus hum ramp). Rain on ductwork. |
| Distant radio | A **squelch tail** (120 ms of white noise, hard-cut), then formant babble from a sawtooth through three wandering bandpasses, band-limited to 300–3,000 Hz. Never intelligible. |
| Score | None, by design. When a key image comes up in the tray, a slow sine cluster tuned to the enlarger's 60 Hz harmonics rises and then *stops with the timer bell*. |

## 5. Three screenshot moments (plus one)
1. **"Lights Out."** Loading Roll 1 cuts the screen to **true black** for up to 90 s. You feel the film onto the reel through sound, clicks and buzz, and the timer's luminous hands are the only light. The tank lid clunks, the lights come up, and the safelight swims into view as the dark adaptation opens. People will clip this. An accessibility "assist" draws faint outlines.
2. **"The Rising."** An 8×10 print rocks in the developer under amber safelight while dithered caustics slide over it. A face comes up shadows first: Mina asleep on the bus (Roll 6), the first time the player sees who this is about.
3. **"Brick Course."** Two frames side by side, each with a red wax horizon line, three courses apart. One image tells the whole twist.
4. *(Bonus)* **"The Window."** Scrub the 8× loupe along Roll 11's contact sheet and three years of seasons flip past in one window. At frame 36, the glass reflects a small woman holding a camera with **white tape on the strap**. On FILE or PURGE, the game composes **the Wall**, a 1-bit corkboard poster of every contact sheet with *your* red marks and string, as the share card.

## 6. Notes for implementers
- **Saves:** `localStorage` (versioned). They store developed rolls (parameters only; negatives regenerate deterministically from a seed plus process parameters), prints, discovered terms, grease-pencil strokes and clock time. There are three slots, labelled as cassette stickers.
- **Clue survival:** keyed to tonal zones, so craft matters but the case can't soft-lock:
  - Each roll can be re-developed once. A "second cassette": the photographer bracketed, which is canon for Ruth and *not* for Mina. Her rolls are one-shot, and the in-world fallback is a dupe negative made at the copy stand.
  - Every essential term is also reachable through a second document.
- **`window.__game`:**
  - `search(term)`
  - `develop(roll, {tempC, minutes, agitation, bzt})`
  - `print(roll, frame, {seconds, grade, dodge[], burn[]})`
  - `setPalette(name)`
  - `revealAll()`
  - `skipDark()`
  - `goto('terminal'|'darkroom'|'wall')`
  - `state()`
