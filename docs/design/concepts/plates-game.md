# HALATION: The Cairn Hollin Plates

**Subtitle:** *Twelve plates from a mountain observatory, winter 1889–90. Develop them. Find out who kept the Hours.*
(Fallback title if "Halation" reads too obscure on the hub card: **The Hollin Plates**.)

Concept by the game-design lens. Slug `plates`. 1-bit dithered 3D, two-tone palettes, period deduction mystery. Every name, place and event below is original.

---

## 1. The pitch in one breath

April 1890. You are the Meteorological Society's examiner, sent up Cairn Hollin, a fictional Highland summit observatory, after the spring relief party found it snowed to the eaves. Inside they found two frozen bodies, a live cat, and an **hourly weather log kept without a single gap until 2 March, in the Superintendent's own hand**. The Superintendent has plainly been dead since December. Beside the log sits a crate of exposed but undeveloped glass plates, taken by the expedition's photographer. The darkroom tank froze in January, so she could never process them.

You thaw the tank and develop the plates one at a time. Each plate you develop becomes a frozen 3D moment you can lean into. You work out who each person was, what became of them, and who kept writing the log after its author died.

**Theme in six words:** *The log is a promise. The plates are the truth.* The lie at the centre is a tender one. Nobody here is a villain. The deduction ends in grief and respect, not a culprit.

---

## 2. Design pillars

1. **Your craft is your evidence.** How well you develop a plate decides how much of the truth you can see. The toy and the mystery are one system.
2. **Physics of photography is the puzzle grammar.** Snow depth, shadow angle, double exposure, handedness, halation and sensitometry are the clues. A clue never depends on a riddle the author made up.
3. **No walls of text.** Instructions come from objects: a wedge strip, a red lamp, a chalk mark, a cat.
4. **Fair and two-witnessed.** Every load-bearing fact appears on at least two plates, or in one plate's midtones, which survive any non-ruinous development.

## 3. Core loop (3–6 minutes per plate)

```
CRATE ──pick holder──▶ DARKROOM (toy) ──fix & wash──▶ LIGHTBOX / VELVET ──▶ STEREOSCOPE (frozen 3D)
   ▲                                                                         │
   └──── TIMELINE (date it) ◀──── CASEBOOK (name, fate, Hour Book) ◀────────┘
```

1. **Crate.** Plate holders have chalk numbers and paper labels in the photographer's hand. Labels are often water-stained, so part of each one is a puzzle.
2. **Darkroom (the toy).** Enter and the palette flips to **ruby on black** (safelight). Slide the dark slide out of the holder, lay the plate in the developer tray and rock it. The tray tilts with a mouse drag or the phone gyro, and the liquid sloshes as a dithered heightfield. The image comes up in the dither as you watch. There is **no numeric timer**. A wall clock ticks and a thermometer hangs in the tray. Then stop bath, then fixer. The plate turns from milky to clear, and only after that is white light safe. Then the wash tank, where on the first visit you break the ice with a tap.
3. **Lightbox.** The negative shows inverted on the opal glass. Flip it onto **black velvet** and it reads as a positive, the period-true ambrotype trick. This is the game's "invert" button, done as a physical gesture.
4. **Stereoscope.** The photographer shot a stereo camera, so every plate is a stereo pair, which makes it a **frozen 3D tableau** with limited parallax (±8° orbit, the "stereo latitude"). Lean around a doorframe to see a face. A brass **loupe** (pinch, or the mouse wheel) magnetically snaps to "noticeables", so there is no pixel hunting. Development flaws are baked into the diorama: a thin plate's shadows stay mud in 3D too.
5. **Casebook and timeline.** Tag people, pin the plate to a week on the winter timeline, and fill the report.

### The diegetic gauge: the H&D wedge
Every plate carries a **pre-flashed 8-step sensitometric wedge** in its rebate. The photographer was a keen follower of the brand-new 1890 Hurter–Driffield work, which is period-true. While you develop, the wedge squares come up one at a time. **Ideal: step 8 just visible, step 1 not yet blocked solid.** That is the whole tutorial for development, and it needs no words. The wedge is also the per-plate craft score and the share artifact (see §8).

### The craft → information rule (the heart of the game)
Each plate's clues are authored into **tonal zones**:

| Development | Shadows (zone II–III) | Midtones | Highlights (zone VII–VIII) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Thin (pulled early / cold developer) | lost | ✓ | ✓ |
| Brilliant (wedge 1–8 distinct) | ✓ | ✓ | ✓ |
| Dense (left too long / too warm) | ✓ | ✓ | blocked |
| Streaked (lazy agitation) | random bromide-drag bands over 1–2 noticeables | | |
| Fogged (white light before fixing) | silhouettes only, ghostly | | |

Midtones carry everything essential, so a mediocre player can always finish. Shadows and highlights carry the **corroborating and bonus** detail: the date on the open log, the time on a clock face, the knit pattern on a cap. A skilled darkroom hand reads the story faster and more surely.

**Remediation, not undo.** The chemistry cabinet holds finite reagents. **Intensifier** (2 uses) rescues a thin plate. **Farmer's reducer** (2 uses: ferricyanide plus hypo, the same yellow salt as cyanotype chemistry, a deliberate rhyme with Game 2) rescues a dense one. Fogged plates cannot be rescued, but they still show silhouettes. A ruined plate is a loss you feel, never a soft-lock.

### Temperature: the observatory is freezing
Developer starts at 8°C, and cold developer is slow. A **paraffin stove** stands in the darkroom corner. Slide the tray onto its warming plate to speed development, but its **red mica window leaks light** unless you close the shutter. Run too warm on the water-damaged Box III plates and the emulsion **frills** (lifts off the glass at the edges). This one physical object carries a small risk/reward system with no UI.

## 4. Story (spoiler section for the build team)

**Cast (6 and a cat):**
- **Corwin Bellamy-Rhys**: Superintendent, 58. Ear trumpet. His life's work is the *Unbroken Hours*, eleven winters of hourly readings without a gap.
- **Thomas Quayle**: Second Observer, 34. Clay pipe, ink-stained cuffs. Loyal to Corwin.
- **Ida Marrable**: Photographer on the Society's cloud-atlas commission. Seldom seen, because she is behind the camera.
- **Hester Dunmore**: Cook-housekeeper. Ring of keys at her waist.
- **Pip Dunmore**: Her son, 13. Lamp boy. Left-handed.
- **Nils Arvesen**: Signalman, runs the heliograph and lamp. Distinctive star-knit cap.
- **Isobar**: The kitten. She grows across the winter, which makes her a timeline clue.

**What happened.** On 27 December Corwin collapses during the night reading in the detached **instrument hut** (heart). The drifts and the frozen ground make burial impossible, so they shut him in the hut. Dying, he made Quayle promise "the Hours will not stop." **Quayle keeps the log in Corwin's hand** from 28 Dec, forging his initials, so the record will not be voided. On 14 Jan Arvesen sets out along the north ridge for help. Ida photographs him through the long lens from the roof. The next day's plate shows the **cornice broken and the tracks ending**. Quayle takes fever and dies in his bunk around 20 Feb. **Pip, whom Quayle taught, keeps the Hours** from 21 Feb to 2 Mar, imitating the same hand but writing left-handed. On 3 Mar, in a thaw window, Ida, Hester and Pip descend the **south glen**. They have to leave Isobar with a sack of oats and an open grain bin. Ida leaves the plates in a crate with a chalk note on the lid. The relief party comes up the north side and misses them entirely.

**Solution table (6 souls):**

| Soul | Fate | Where | When |
|---|---|---|---|
| Corwin Bellamy-Rhys | Died, heart | Instrument hut | Wk of 23 Dec |
| Thomas Quayle | Died, fever | His bunk | Wk of 17 Feb |
| Nils Arvesen | Died, fall | North cornice | Wk of 13 Jan |
| Hester Dunmore | Survived, descended | South glen | Wk of 3 Mar |
| Pip Dunmore | Survived, descended | South glen | Wk of 3 Mar |
| Ida Marrable | Survived, descended | South glen | Wk of 3 Mar |

**The Hour Book question:** "Who kept the Hours?" has three spans. Corwin until 27 Dec, **Quayle** 28 Dec–20 Feb, **Pip** 21 Feb–2 Mar.

## 5. The plates (MVP: 12 + tutorial + secret; architected for 24)

Plates come in **three boxes**. Each box adds one darkroom wrinkle, so the difficulty curve sits in the toy and not in menus. Box n+1 opens once every plate in Box n is *pinned to the timeline* (pinned, not necessarily correct). Gating is low-friction and keeps the arc paced.

The **snow pole** is a painted depth gauge outside the window. It shows in most plates and is the backbone of the timeline: depth climbs until March, then falls. Players learn to read it from plate 1, where it reads zero.

**Box I: Arrival** (normal plates; teaches the wedge, velvet and stereoscope)
- **0. Calibration (tutorial).** Wedge, a test chart, and the kitten asleep in a plate box. Mistakes cost nothing.
- **1. Group portrait at the door, 2 Oct.** Six people, snow pole at 0. The label reads "L to R:" and the names are water-damaged. Only "…uayle", "Mrs D…" and "…ip" survive, so the first deduction is immediate.
- **2. Barograph room.** Corwin and Quayle. The clock reads 9:00 (highlight zone). The open log shows the *handwriting baseline*, Corwin's looped capital B, readable at Brilliant only.
- **3. Kitchen.** Hester, Pip with the kitten, Arvesen mending the signal lamp. The star cap is the identity clue. The window pole reads 14".
- **4. Christmas, magnesium flash.** All six. Corwin grey, hand to chest (foreshadowing). The flash makes the lamp flames **halate**, blooming into the dither, and the photographer is reflected in a halating window: the first sight of Ida.

**Box II: The Hour Book** (introduces **flash/night plates**: high contrast, so the reducer matters)
- **5. 27 Dec, night.** Lantern-lit instrument hut, door ajar, a slumped figure in the **shadow zone**. The second witness is plate 6.
- **6. 28 Dec, dawn.** The hut door shut, Quayle and Arvesen holding shovels, Hester with a blanket. Pole 33".
- **7. "The Ghost," Jan.** The common room with a translucent Corwin at the desk, the period's spirit-photograph scare. The truth is a **double exposure**: the window shows **two superimposed snow poles, at 33" and 41"**. Spotting the double pole flips a casebook card from "Corwin alive in January?" to "Plate reused." This is the game's best "aha", and it runs purely on photographic physics.
- **8. 14 Jan, telephoto from the roof.** A tiny figure on the north ridge. The loupe brings up the star cap. The clouds are lenticular, and the log records "lenticular, 11h", which pins the hour.

**Box III: Thaw** (introduces **water-damaged plates**: warm developer frills the emulsion)
- **9. 15 Jan, same telephoto framing.** The cornice is broken and the tracks end. No body is shown, so the fate is inferred.
- **10. Feb, night.** Quayle at the desk copying from an *older* log page beside Corwin's spectacles. Through the window, the drift against the hut door is **undisturbed**, yet tonight's log entry claims "C.B-R. read hut instruments 21h." That is the contradiction.
- **11. ~21 Feb, 03:00.** Quayle in his bunk with Hester kneeling. Pip writes in the log **with his left hand**, at an hourly-observation time.
- **12. 3 Mar.** Hester and Pip in snowshoes at the door. Ida's presence shows as **her own long shadow with the tripod** on the snow. Isobar watches from the window. The pole has fallen to 40", which means thaw.
- **13. Secret: "the plate in the camera."** The roof camera's dark slide was never pulled. Examine the camera in the observatory and develop it: Ida's self-portrait holding a chalk slate that reads *"To whoever develops these: we went south. Feed the cat."* It unlocks the true epilogue.

**Stretch to 24:** two plates per box of slice-of-life (cloud studies with a figure in the foreground, Pip's birthday, the heliograph flashing to the valley) that thicken identity clues and kitten growth. Plus a **second double exposure** that is *not* a ghost, a skill check that the player really learned the trick.

## 6. Deduction UX: the Society Report

- **Souls:** six lines, each with **Name** (portrait picked from the roster), **Fate** (Died / Descended / Unknown), **Where** (a small set of tagged places), and **When** (week chips on the timeline).
- **Confirmation in pairs.** When any two lines are fully correct, both get a **red wax seal** with a satisfying thunk-and-drip sound. This prevents brute-forcing and gives a heartbeat of reward through the middle hours. (It is a genre convention, so the threshold differs and the presentation is our own.)
- **The Hour Book:** drag ink-coloured bars over the log's date strip to say who wrote each span. It seals when all three spans are right.
- **Timeline:** dating plates is advisory, not scored for confirmation, but each plate placed within its correct week adds to the final grade.
- **Hints: the cat.** After a few minutes without a new seal, **Isobar wanders into the scene and sits on the crate beside the plate that holds your next missing clue**, then grooms herself. Click her for one handwritten marginal line from Ida, such as *"The pole never lies about the day."* No hint button and no shame.

## 7. Failure and success states

- **Failure is local and physical.** Fogging is a dramatic moment: white floods the dither in a wipe, with a sharp *tink*. Frilling peels at the plate's edges. A dense plate is a black slab with a ghost of an image in it. **None of these ends the game.** The two-witness rule guarantees the report can still be finished.
- **Submitting the report:** you can seal it at any time. The verdict is graded in period voice: *Accepted with Distinction* (6/6 souls, Hour Book sealed), *Accepted*, or *Returned for Further Inquiry*. The last one lets you keep playing.
- **Success, the colour beat:** the whole game is two-tone, with no grey and no colour. After sealing, you carry plate 12 to the roof, contact-print it on **cyanotype paper** in April sun, and wash it. **The first and only colour in the game blooms: Prussian blue.** Then a telegram slides under the door: *"THREE ARRIVED GLEN AVIE 6 MARCH STOP WELL STOP."* With plate 13 found, a second line follows: *"CAT?"* This ties directly to Andrew's cyanotype note and to Game 2.

## 8. Scoring and sharing

**Final card** = Report grade × Craft × Time.
- **Craft:** each plate's wedge is graded *Brilliant / Good / Thin / Dense / Streaked / Fogged / Frilled*.
- **The share artifact is the wedge barcode.** A canvas-generated 1-bit PNG stacks the 8-step wedge strips of all your plates, as you actually developed them, into a **contact sheet of barcodes**. It is unique to each player, contains no spoilers and is beautiful. It comes with a text version:

```
HALATION · Cairn Hollin
souls 6/6 · hours ✓ · 1h38m
▁▃▅▆▇██▇  ▂▃▅▆▇▇██  ▁▂▃▅▆▇▇█ …
Accepted with Distinction
```

## 9. Replay and daily hooks

- **The Daily Plate (3 minutes).** A seeded generator builds one tableau from the same kit (room, people, props, clock, pole, kitten) with a **random exposure level**, so the ideal development time differs every day and you must read the wedge. Develop it by eye, then answer one question drawn from a pool: *What time is on the clock? How many people? Which hand holds the pen? Is this a double exposure?* Score = wedge distance from ideal + answer. Share: `Daily Plate No. 142 · ▂▃▅▆▇██▇ · ✓ · 0:52`. There is one attempt per day, and failure is fine and funny.
- **Examiner vs Apprentice.** *Apprentice* allows unlimited rewinds of each development, a "save state" honouring Andrew's note, with no craft score. *Examiner* has one shot per plate and full scoring. The choice is made on the crate lid at start.
- **The Second Crate (New Game+).** The same story with plates exposed differently (underexposed night plates), half the reagents, and the timeline hidden until the end.
- **Spare plates (toy mode).** After plate 1, a "spare" box lets you **import a PNG** (via the shared import module) as a latent plate and develop, fog, frill and velvet-flip it at will, then export the dithered print. The toy is always one click away.

## 10. Onboarding: the first 180 seconds, zero text boxes

1. Wind audio. The 1-bit scene fades in on the desk, the crate and one holder whose chalk "0" shimmers in the dither. The only verb is *pick up*.
2. The darkroom door has a red lamp above it. Walk in and the palette turns to ruby: **ruby means safe**, taught by colour alone.
3. The wash tank is iced over. Tap, crack, splash. The first toy moment comes before any instruction.
4. Plate 0 goes in the tray. The wedge squares appear and the kitten in the picture emerges. If you over-develop, the kitten vanishes into black. Players learn the rule by losing the cat, at no cost.
5. A white-light switch on the wall carries a small hand-painted **"after the fix"** sign, the one piece of text, set as a period stencil.
6. Velvet flip, then the stereoscope. Leaning reveals the kitten's tail behind the box, which teaches parallax.
7. Plate 1 and the half-washed label. The casebook opens itself to the roster. The game has begun.

## 12. Build notes the design depends on (for the synthesizer)

- **Plates are data.** `plates.json` entries: `{id, box, kitScene, cameraPose, stereoLatitudeDeg, actors:[{who,pose,pos}], props, snowPole, cloud, clock, kittenScale, exposureEV, damage:'none'|'water', doubleExposureOf?, noticeables:[{id, zone:'shadow'|'mid'|'highlight', clueTag}]}`. 12 plates come from one kit of ~4 rooms, 6 low-poly posable figures and ~25 props.
- **Development is a post-process on the 3D render:** linear luminance → latent → `density = 1 - exp(-k·t·f(temp)·L^γ)` + agitation-noise streak mask + fog term → threshold against blue-noise/Bayer → two-tone palette. The developed state is a uniform set saved per plate, so the stereoscope view re-renders the 3D with *your* development applied.
- **`window.__game`:** `listPlates()`, `loadPlate(id)`, `develop(seconds, tempC, agitation)`, `fix()`, `fog()`, `wedge(id)` → `number[8]`, `setView('darkroom'|'lightbox'|'stereo'|'casebook')`, `orbit(deg)`, `report.set(soul, fields)`, `report.seals()`, `solveAll()`, `seedDaily(n)`.
- **Degrade gracefully:** without WebGL2, the stereoscope falls back to pre-rendered still dithers at three development levels per plate, generated at runtime on a 2D canvas from cached renders where possible. The darkroom tray works fully in 2D canvas.

**The one-line test for every feature:** *does it make the player look harder at a photograph?* If not, cut it.
