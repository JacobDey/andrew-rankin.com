# SAFELIGHT: a darkroom toy for silver and sun

*Game 2, slug `darkroom`. Concept through the **game design** lens.*

**Title:** SAFELIGHT
**Subtitle:** *a hands-in-the-tray darkroom for silver and sun*
(Fallback if the hub wants plain names: **Darkroom: Silver & Iron**. Silver gelatin and iron-based cyanotype are the game's two halves.)

---

## 1. The one-sentence game

You're handed a negative and a brief. You have a room full of light switches, liquids, and paper that remembers every photon. Make the print the brief asks for, or make a better one by accident.

## 2. Design pillars (the test for every feature)

1. **The print is the score.** No abstract points, coins or XP. Every judgment is a measurement of the physical print you made: density, fog, evenness, contrast. The print hangs on the line forever, good or bad.
2. **Every mistake is a technique somebody named.** Fogging, uneven development and flashing the light mid-development are all failure modes, and each is also a real darkroom technique (pre-flashing, solarization, chemigrams). The game punishes them in one brief and asks for them in another. This is how the toy becomes a game without losing the toy.
3. **The room is the UI.** No HUD. The interface is the wind-up dial timer, grease-pencil notes on the wall, labels on the jugs, the clipboard log and the clothesline. Text never exceeds what fits on a strip of masking tape.
4. **Wait only where waiting is the pleasure.** Watching an image bloom in developer runs in real time. Fixing, washing and drying are compressed, skippable or turned into small toys.

## 3. The four systems puzzles come from

All challenges come from four simulated systems. A brief is just a set of starting conditions plus a target print.

| System | What the player touches | What it produces |
|---|---|---|
| **Light** | Room switch, safelight bulb (red/amber/white, can be unscrewed and moved), enlarger lamp + lens cap, door (light spill), blackout curtain on the window (sun!), UV box, the player's own hand | Exposure, fog, dodge/burn shadows, sun printing |
| **Liquid** | Trays you tilt and slosh (drag a corner on desktop, device tilt on phones with drag fallback), jugs, droppers, wash sink, peroxide spray bottle, brush | Where development actually happens: streaks, coverage, bromide drag, uneven coating |
| **Chemistry** | Developer, stop, fixer, wash; cyanotype A + B, peroxide, tea, washing soda | Development rate, exhaustion and oxidation (developer turns tea-brown), under-fixing, toning color |
| **Time** | Dial timer, real-world clock (for sun strength and aging), the clothesline | Over- and under-development, safelight fog creep, prints that change days later |

**The great rule flip.** Silver paper must stay in the dark: room light fogs it instantly and the safelight fogs it slowly after about 4 minutes. Cyanotype paper is UV-sensitive and doesn't care about the room light, so you coat it with the lights *on* and expose it in the *sun*. The first time the player opens the blackout curtain on purpose is the midpoint "aha" of the campaign. The room is the same, but the rules have inverted.

## 4. Core loops

**Silver loop (2-4 min):**
Read brief, then lights off and safelight on. Slide the negative into the enlarger and focus with a grain magnifier (a satisfying snap to sharp). Make a test strip. Expose, dodging and burning with your hand or card. Developer: slosh and watch the image bloom in real time, about 60-90 s. Stop. Fixer: 15 s compressed, slosh to keep it even. Wash, then lights on and judge. Clip the print to the line.

**Cyanotype loop (5-8 min, deliberately slower, "the Sunday loop"):**
Mix A + B with droppers (equal parts; the color tells you the ratio is off). Brush-coat the paper; the strokes stay visible as character. Dry it on the line or with the hair dryer (too hot gives streaks). Prepare a digital negative from an imported PNG or a built-in image (invert, grayscale, curve). Sandwich it under glass, open the curtain, and watch the paper go yellow-green, then grey, then bronze. *Pull it at bronze*: a timing skill read from color, not a meter. Wash it and watch the yellow run out while the blue appears. A peroxide spritz gives the instant deep-blue **pop**, the game's most satisfying moment. Optionally tone it: washing soda bleach, then tea, for brown-black.

**Meta loop:** Brief, then print, then critique, then pin or retry, then clothesline, then the notebook (technique discovered?), then the next brief or the Open Darkroom.

## 5. Onboarding: the previous tenant's clothesline

The game opens in a cramped bathroom darkroom with the **room light on**. Six prints hang from a clothesline, left by "the previous tenant," who appears only as initials and handwriting. Every one of them is ruined, and each has a masking-tape tag:

- a grey wash of nothing: *"light on. 2 sec."*
- streaky, half-developed: *"didn't rock the tray"*
- brown and stained: *"skipped the fixer. it's still going."* (It slowly darkens over real days. Foreshadowing.)
- a gorgeous print with a ghostly reversed sky: *"left the door open. KEEP THIS ONE."* (Solarization, the first hint that mistakes are techniques.)

The ruined prints are the tutorial, and the tags are the only text. Tapping a print shows its darkroom log strip (see section 8), which replays the mistake as a 3-second ghost.

**The first 90 seconds:**
1. A grease-pencil arrow on the wall points at the switch. Flip it. The room goes red and the safelight hums. (One verb.)
2. A sheet of already-exposed paper is waiting by the tray, so there's no enlarger yet. Drag it in, and the image blooms within 20 seconds. *The magic moment happens before any rules have been explained.*
3. The tongs drag it to the next tray. The tray labels STOP and FIX are the instructions.
4. Lights on. The print is a slightly sloppy picture of a rusty mailbox (a nod to Texture-dle). It clips to the line next to the tenant's failures. You're now part of the line.

Each later verb arrives with exactly one job: enlarger in job 3, test strip in job 4, filters in job 6. If the player flips the room light with the paper box open, only the **top sheet** fogs (the box is forgiving) and the game hangs it on the line with an auto-tag in the same handwriting style: *"you too, huh."*

## 6. Campaign: four rooms, 16 jobs

The darkroom grows between chapters. You go from bathroom to proper room, the window gets uncovered, and finally a gallery wall appears. Jobs come as briefs pinned to a corkboard: a small target print plus one line of client voice. All clients are original and appear only as handwriting.

| # | Job | Teaches | Twist |
|---|---|---|---|
| **I. The Bathroom** ||||
| 1 | *First Light* | Developer, tongs | Paper is pre-exposed |
| 2 | *Lights Out* | Light discipline, the paper box | Box lid is physical; opening it in light fogs the top sheet |
| 3 | *Contact* | Exposure time, enlarger as a lamp | Contact sheet of 6 frames; the brief wants frame 4 "properly" |
| 4 | *Test Strip* | Stepped exposure with a sliding card | Pass = within ±1/3 stop of target |
| 5 | *The Leak* | **Environmental detective puzzle** | Prints have faint fog. Find the leak: light under the door (stuff the towel), a phone glowing face-up on the bench (flip it), the safelight too close (move it). Each fix is a tactile act. |
| **II. The Enlarger** ||||
| 6 | *Hard Sky* | Contrast filters (grades 00-5) | Match the target's contrast, not its brightness |
| 7 | *Hold Still* | Dodging with your hand | Face in shadow. A still hand leaves a hard halo; **wiggle** to feather it, and the game shows the difference |
| 8 | *Burn the Window* | Burning in with a card with a hole | Blown-out window in an interior shot |
| 9 | *Half a Liter* | Liquid physics mastery | Only enough developer to cover 60% of the tray floor. Rock it or get a dead corner. |
| 10 | *Ten in a Row* | Chemistry exhaustion | Ten prints from one tray of developer. Replenish wisely; the bottle oxidizes if you leave air in it (squeeze the accordion bottle). |
| **III. Blue** ||||
| 11 | *Blue Hour* | Cyanotype: mix, coat, dry, sun | **The curtain opens.** Rule flip. |
| 12 | *Leaves* | Photogram: lay objects on the paper | Ferns, keys, a lace doily. No negative needed. |
| 13 | *Your Own Light* | PNG import plus negative prep | Ships with a built-in image if the player imports nothing. The target is "a full tonal range", scored on tonal spread, not likeness. |
| 14 | *Pop* | Peroxide, then toning with tea | Target is brown-black, not blue |
| **IV. The Wall** ||||
| 15 | *Flash* | Solarization, on purpose | The tenant's "KEEP THIS ONE" as a brief. Flash the room light at about 40% development. Timing window ~1.5 s. |
| 16 | *The Show* | Curation | Pick 5 prints from your clothesline for the gallery wall. They're scored only for *variety of technique* (the notebook), never for perfection. This ending can't be failed. |

Optional **Chemigram** job: paint with developer and fixer in room light to match an abstract shape. It unlocks when the player first touches developer with a brush.

## 7. Scoring: the print is the score

When you turn the lights on, a **densitometer probe** and a **loupe** appear on the bench. The critique uses five measured quantities. Each is computed from the print's density field against the target:

- **Zone match**: an 8×8 grid of mean densities compared with the target after normalization. This is the headline percentage.
- **Blacks (Dmax)**: deepest density reached. Weak blacks mean underdevelopment or exhausted developer.
- **Paper white**: fog in the borders. Safelight creep shows up here.
- **Evenness**: variance in development coverage. This catches slosh and coating failures.
- **Hand**: a bonus for detectable intentional techniques (dodge, burn, flash, toning) used where the brief wants them.

Grades use darkroom words: **Proof** (≥60%), **Work Print** (≥80%), **Exhibition** (≥92% with no flaw flags). Below Proof the print still hangs; it just doesn't clear the brief. Feedback is three short margin notes from the measurements, in the critic's handwriting: *"blacks are muddy, tired developer?"*, *"lovely sky"*, *"drag marks bottom edge, rock the tray."* Each note names what to fix, so the critique is also the tutorial.

## 8. Failure as forensics: the darkroom log

Every print stores a compact **log**: light state over time, which tray at which second, agitation intensity, chemical freshness. It's drawn as a thin timeline strip clipped under the print, with red ticks for fog events and grey bands for under-agitation. Tap a red tick and a 3-second ghost replay shows the room at that moment, with the door ajar and light spilling across the bench. Mistakes become stories you can investigate, which matters because the photographic chain hides cause and effect. This replaces tutorials for everything after chapter I.

**Delayed consequences.** An under-fixed print looks fine today. Prints are saved with real timestamps, and over the next 1-3 real days an under-fixed print slowly stains on the clothesline. The game never warns you. When you come back, the log shows *"fix: 4 s"* with a red tick. It's the single best "the simulation is real" moment and a natural return hook.

## 9. The notebook of happy accidents (collection meta)

The notebook is the progression track: about 16 technique entries, each **discovered by doing it**, usually by accident. When the simulation detects a signature (for example, reversal in mid-tones after a light event during development), a page writes itself in: *"Discovered: Solarization. You flashed the light 38 s into development."* Entries include fogging and pre-flashing, solarization, bromide drag, dodging, burning, split-grade, test strip, photogram, chemigram, cyanotype, bronzing, peroxide pop, tea toning, bleach-back, double exposure and lumen-style print-out (paper left in the sun with no developer slowly prints itself). Locked entries show only a smudged thumbnail. Discovery in the Open Darkroom counts, so play in the toy feeds the game.

## 10. Save states: pins and branching prints

Andrew asked for save states. They're diegetic. A **pushpin** tool lets you pin the whole room state (paper, chemistry, light, time, wet print in progress) to the corkboard as a Polaroid-style thumbnail of the room. Load a pin and you're back at that moment.

**The twist: prints are outside time.** Loading a pin doesn't remove prints already on the clothesline. Pin before toning, tone with tea, reload, tone with selenium-style bleach, reload, leave it blue, and the line now holds three siblings with a small branch glyph linking them. Save-scumming *is* experimentation, and the game celebrates it.

The campaign allows pins freely. **Daily** and **One Sheet** modes disable them. Pins are stored in IndexedDB: room state as JSON plus the print density fields as compressed Float16 or 8-bit textures.

## 11. Replay and daily hooks

- **Tonight's Negative (daily).** A date-seeded procedural negative (still lifes, mailboxes, street corners, fern fronds) plus a target print with a hidden trick: a dodge here, a grade-4 sky there, sometimes a flash. You get **one sheet of paper** and test strips are allowed (strips are free and teach without spoiling). There are no pins. Your score is the zone-match %. After judging, a ghost of the target fades in over your print.
- **Real sun.** In cyanotype jobs the sun's UV strength comes from the player's local time and timezone, a rough solar-elevation model needing no network. Midday exposures take 40 s of game time, evening ones 3 minutes, and after dark the curtain shows streetlights and you must use the UV box. Clouds drift across procedurally; a cloud mid-exposure is a small drama. Players will notice it's actually sunny in the game when it's sunny outside.
- **Aging chemistry.** Developer left in an open tray browns over real hours, so the room you return to has a history. Fresh chemistry is one tap at the sink, so this adds flavor, not chores.
- **Stained fingers.** Grab paper by hand instead of with tongs and the cursor gets brown fingertips (developer) or blue ones (cyanotype) that fade over a real day. It's cosmetic and silly, and people will screenshot it.
- **One Sheet (mastery mode).** Any completed job replayed with one sheet of paper, no pins, no test strips. This is where Exhibition grades get earned.

## 12. Sharing

**Share string** (Wordle-style, from your print's 6×4 zone grid):

```
SAFELIGHT · Night 142 · 91% Exhibition
▓▓▒▒░░
▓▒▒░░░
█▓▒▒▒░
██▓▓▒▒
1 sheet · 2 strips · 3:12 · solarized
```

The block grid is *your print*, so every share is a tiny abstract artwork. **Share card PNG:** your print matted on cream paper with film-rebate edge markings encoding the day number, grade and techniques, as a downloadable image. The rebate is a procedurally generated "frame number" font, an original typeface rather than any brand's markings.

## 13. Pacing

- **Minutes 0-2:** first print, magic moment, no rules spoken.
- **Minutes 2-15:** chapter I. One new verb per job, each job 1-3 minutes.
- **Chapter II:** skill depth with the enlarger, deliberately "fiddly-fun." This is where the hands play.
- **Chapter III:** tempo slows. The cyanotype loop is calmer, sunnier and more meditative; the soundtrack shifts from safelight hum to window birds.
- **Chapter IV:** payoff. Your mistakes become techniques; your clothesline becomes a show.

**Time compression:** developer is always real time. Fixer is 15 s. Washing is a running tap you can leave while you start the next exposure (parallelism adds busy-darkroom flow). Drying is the clothesline: it happens while you do other things, or the hair dryer forces it. The dial timer can be **cranked forward** by hand for waits; it's tactile and costs nothing in the toy, but One Sheet logs it.

## 14. Open Darkroom (the toy)

The Open Darkroom is available from the main menu **after the first print** (90 seconds in) and is never gated behind the campaign. Gating a sandbox is a crime against toys. It contains everything you've seen, with one exception: under the doormat is a key labelled *"everything"*, the sandbox unlock for people who just want to play. Open Darkroom feeds the notebook, clothesline and pins. It's where imported PNGs live permanently, in a "negatives binder" in IndexedDB.

## 15. Cross-game hooks (cheap, high-delight)

A shared `src/shared/filmcan` module (localStorage/IndexedDB) where any game drops "exposed film": Lensbench and Studio photos, or Texture-dle's daily texture. Darkroom's binder shows a **film can** icon with a count. Develop your Lensbench vacation photos here. Plates (the Obra Dinn-styled game) and Latent can reuse the same density-field print renderer with a different dither and palette.

## 16. Scope tiers (for a 2-4 agent, 1-2 hour build)

- **Must:** room with light states (switch, safelight, enlarger, door); 3 trays with tilt-slosh 2D height-field liquid; paper density field developed only where liquid touches; fog model; test strip; dodge by hand; scoring plus 3 margin notes; clothesline; jobs 1-7 and 11-13; daily with share string; pins (save states); PNG import with invert/grayscale/curve.
- **Should:** contrast filters, exhaustion and oxidation, darkroom log with ghost replay, notebook (8 entries), peroxide pop, tea toning, real-sun model, delayed under-fix staining.
- **Stretch:** solarization job, chemigram, The Show, stained fingers, phone tilt via DeviceOrientation (iOS permission prompt behind a "tilt mode" button), film-can cross-game import.

## 17. `window.__game` (design-facing hooks)

`loadJob(n)`, `loadDaily(seed)`, `setLight({room, safelight, enlarger, curtain})`, `exposeSeconds(s)`, `dunk(tray, seconds, agitation)`, `skipTime(s)`, `scorePrint() -> {zone, dmax, white, evenness, hand, grade, notes}`, `pin()`, `loadPin(id)`, `getShareString()`, `setSunHour(h)`. With these, Playwright can play a full job end-to-end and screenshot a scored print, so every brief can be regression-tested for solvability: each job ships with a scripted reference solution that must reach Exhibition.
